## The 90-second field update (open with this)

Since April the question stopped being hypothetical. Three lines converge: (1) *Winters v. OpenAI* — a disposition-failure suit, allegations only; (2) the *Nature Medicine* ChatGPT Health stress test — 51.6% of gold-standard emergencies undertriaged, anchoring OR 11.7 — now publicly contested as partly an artifact of its forced-choice format, with the original group's own follow-ups showing multi-turn chat does **not** fix alignment and the same benchmark run on OpenEvidence failing four-fold less; (3) randomized evidence (Bean; NOHARM's 101-physician RCT) that the human–AI interface gives back much of what models do alone, and NOHARM's finding that safety correlates only r = 0.61–0.64 with knowledge benchmarks. The field is now arguing about **how to measure** consumer triage safety. Our design — free-response, dual-register, physician-benchmarked, severity-weighted, interaction-inclusive — is the instrument both sides' arguments call for, and the consumer-disposition standard slot is still open.

## Anticipated questions — answers in one breath

- *"Weren't you scooped by the Nature Medicine paper?"* — It proved our premise and left the standard unbuilt: one product, one timepoint, one run per cell, forced-choice elicitation, no physician comparator, no register manipulation, no interaction arm. Its authors say deployment "should not rest on trust alone"; its critics say valid evaluation "requires conditions that reflect actual use." Both describe this protocol.
- *"Isn't the failure just the evaluation format?"* — Partly, and that is the point: same format on OpenEvidence failed four-fold less, and free-text multi-turn didn't rescue ChatGPT Health. Only a free-response, physician-benchmarked design can adjudicate — ours scores escalation deterministically from the model's own words.
- *"How is this different from NOHARM?"* — Different construct: clinician-facing consultation comprehensiveness vs consumer disposition safety. We adopt its best methods (harm anchors, autograder acceptance bar, floor/ceiling comparators) and test what it explicitly could not: patient-facing registers and real interaction.
- *"Why Cedars, why now?"* — 43 citations on Ramaswamy in five months, a live methods dispute, three benchmark groups already staked (Mount Sinai, Macquarie, Stanford). The consumer-disposition standard is unclaimed; the ED physicians who own the triage boundary should define it.
- *"What's the burden?"* — No patient data; de-identified composites; minimal-risk physician participation, ~6–10 hours per person; 22-week timeline.
