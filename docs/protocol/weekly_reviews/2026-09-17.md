## What was found — the week's material finding

**Samsel K, Dharma C, Rezaei AHM, et al. "Too agreeable to be accurate? Sycophancy and diagnostic
instability of large language models in medical diagnosis." *Artif Intell Med* 2026;182:103516.
doi 10.1016/j.artmed.2026.103516, PMID 42721584.** University of Toronto. Peer-reviewed.
120 vignettes × 10 models × 4 prompt conditions × 3 runs × 2 passes = 28,800 responses.

A single "Are you sure?" moved accuracy from **51.8% to 42.2%**, and the flips were **asymmetric**:
544 correct-to-incorrect against 198 incorrect-to-correct. The worst-affected model lost 36.4
percentage points. Specialty framing moved things far less — the certainty challenge was the
strongest single source of instability in the study.

### The gap this exposes in our own protocol → EDIT 1

§5.5 Arm A elicits **one** response per scenario. §4.1 scores the Critical Miss Rate from that
response. §10 lists three boundaries — the S2/S3 encounter-time tail, vignettes-are-not-patients,
and benchmark-is-not-clearance — and **none of them is the single-turn boundary.**

A reader can finish §10 believing every limitation has been disclosed and still not know that the
primary endpoint is measured on one model turn, and that the most ordinary thing a worried patient
does next — asking "are you sure?" — is outside what the instrument observes.

"Vignettes are not patients" does not cover this. That boundary is about how the patient
*presents*: information loss, emotional pressure, partial disclosure. The single-turn boundary is
about what happens *after the model answers*. Different exposure, and now one with a measured
magnitude attached.

This is the same class of finding as the 3 September refusal-category gap: not an error in the
protocol, but a property of the design that a reader is entitled to see stated.

**What EDIT 1 deliberately does not do.** It adds no measurement, no arm, no scenario, no
participant burden, and has **no effect on the §8.4 power calculation** that is already blocking
filing. It is a disclosure, not a design change.

**Honesty about transfer, written into the proposed text.** Samsel measured *diagnostic accuracy*
flips on general clinical vignettes — not *triage disposition* flips on emergency scenarios under
an asymmetric loss function. The magnitude must not be assumed to transfer and the edit says so.
What transfers is the direction: a single-turn Critical Miss Rate is an **upper bound** on the
safety of a system a patient can talk back to.

EDIT 1 also carries a small third hunk clarifying §4.2's run-to-run instability metric — it
measures nondeterminism under a *fixed* prompt, which is a different quantity from
challenge-induced flipping, and the two should not be conflated by a reader who sees "instability"
in both places.

## Put to the PI as an option — deliberately NOT proposed as an edit

Samsel et al. recommend evaluations report "stability under clinically plausible conversational
challenges." This protocol is precisely the predeployment instrument that recommendation targets,
so the option is surfaced rather than omitted — but it is a **design expansion**, so it is not
written as a patch hunk.

It would mean a pre-specified second turn on a subset of Arm A scenarios carrying a fixed neutral
challenge, scored with the same phrase-inventory rules, reported as harmful flips versus beneficial
corrections. **The cost is real: it expands the §8.4 power calculation, which is the blocking
item.** It also overlaps Arm C1 (`[DECISION 4]`). Recommendation: decide it *together with*
DECISION 4, not separately.

## What was deliberately not proposed

- **The case-source effect is NOT cited for our register factor.** Samsel found accuracy lower on
  MultiCaRe narratives than MedMCQA exam cases, which looks like support for our dual-register
  probe. It is not clean support: MedMCQA is multiple-choice and MultiCaRe is open-ended, so the
  contrast confounds input genre with *answer* format, while our register factor varies the input
  and holds free-text answering constant. It also runs opposite to the standing Fraile Navarro
  alert (naturalistic phrasing *improved* accuracy 6.4pp). Both can hold because they vary
  different things; citing Samsel here would blur exactly that.
- **Stankić et al.** rules-based vs LLM triage chatbot (*Health Informatics J*, PMID 42727093) —
  library only. Rules-based most accurate (71.8%) yet testers preferred the LLM versions; authors
  name "masking of poorer performance by conversational fluency." Supports choices the protocol has
  already made; n = 13 testers is thin, and no protocol claim turns on it.
- **Zuo et al.** TBI/concussion audit (*Front Public Health*, PMID 42745758) — library only. 27 of
  325 responses potentially harmful, mainly under-triage and overly reassuring advice. Corroborates
  the premise; adds no mechanism we do not already handle.
- **Xiao et al.** late-life depression benchmark (*Front Psychiatry*, doi 10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1956736)
  — library only and **quarantined as NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED this cycle.** The reconciler added
  it 13 Sep marked "scite-verified"; it has no PMID, and Scite is returning unrelated records, so I
  could not re-verify it. Its figures — including high-risk under-triage of 16.7% / 26.7% / 36.7% —
  are **unverified here and cited nowhere.** It is `design_overlap = true` and a close
  methodological cousin (physician-adjudicated, risk-stratified, blinded, test-retest subset),
  differentiated by domain. If the PI wants it for methods differentiation, it needs verification
  first.
- **Two records could not be retrieved** and nothing is quoted or reconstructed from either:
