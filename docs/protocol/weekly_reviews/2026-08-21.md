## Proposed edits — **PI APPROVED 1–4, 21 Aug 2026** ("APPROVE EDITS 1-4"); applied and pushed same day (`proposed_edits_2026-08-21.patch`)

1. **URGENT — a novelty claim is now falsified and should not stand in an IRB-bound document.** The Scientific Introduction stated twice
   that the note-embedded cue locus "remains unexamined in any published study." Omar et al.
   (*Rheumatology* 2026, PMID 42587430 — peer-reviewed, 200k+ queries, 5 LLMs) show a dismissive
   clinician anchor in the referral note significantly reduces acuity concordance (p=0.01), chiefly by
   downgrading urgency; MTS-Bench (preprint) shows a misleading GP-referral statement drove GPT-5.1 to
   undertriage 8/8 Red cases. Proposed revision cites-and-differentiates: the locus is now
   demonstrated *outside* patient-facing emergency triage, which is the setting this protocol tests.
2. **Add contrasting register evidence for equipoise.** Ada et al. (*EJOG* 2026, PMID 42561577):
   matched patient- vs clinician-language OB/GYN triage prompts showed **no significant register
   effect** (OR 0.78, p=0.115), lay phrasing if anything safer. Proposed edit makes the prediction carry this contrast explicitly — the register factor resolves it in
   the emergency disposition domain.
3. **Strengthen the contamination section with observed propagation.** The Ramaswamy 60-vignette set was
   reused within six months by two further evaluations (Gokhale UMLS-CUI mitigation, preprint;
   MTS-Bench adaptation, preprint) — direct evidence for the de novo / private-split / embedded-probe
   design.
4. **Add Key References 19–21** (Omar; Ada; MTS-Bench/Sheppert) and an evidence-weight note under
   DECISION 1 in the protocol's completion register.

- **[MODERATE] Cite-and-differentiate MTS-Bench in the "Closest Prior Work" section.** It is the
  nearest design neighbour to date (ordered-scale undertriage endpoint + anchoring manipulation), but:
  reused scenarios (not de novo), single model, no physician answering panel, no lay register, no
  severity weights, preprint. A paragraph placing it would pre-empt "how is this different from
  MTS-Bench?" at review.
- **[MODERATE] Note Sheppert et al. (IJMI 2026, PMID 42335861) for the anchoring hypothesis.** LLMs
  ranked a patient-suggested benign anchor first ~4x more often than physicians (55.6% vs 21.2%
  residents / 10.0% attendings) — a human-comparator anchor result *within* the forced-choice paradigm,
  which also bears on the Fraile Navarro dispute. Added to Key References; consider it for the deck.
- **[WATCH] Weilnhammer et al. (arXiv, Microsoft/Oxford; alert of 6 Aug).** One-shot emergency
  psychiatric triage across 15 chatbots, 5.6% emergency undertriage — bears on the exploratory
  crisis-safeguard endpoint. No action needed until the pilot.
- **[WATCH] Gokhale UMLS-CUI mitigation (preprint; alert of 15 Aug).** First mitigation study on the
  Ramaswamy harness; reduced undertriage but *increased* overtriage — live illustration of why the
  protocol reports a never-escalate floor and always-escalate ceiling. Possible future intervention-arm
