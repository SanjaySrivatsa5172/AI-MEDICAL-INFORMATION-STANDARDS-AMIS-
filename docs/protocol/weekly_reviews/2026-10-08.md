## The week's material finding — and it is the only peer-reviewed one

**Pichowicz W, Kotas M, Kalka N, et al. "Responses of AI chatbots to escalating suicide risk: A
simulation study of repeated interactions." *J Affect Disord* 2026;122586.
doi 10.1016/j.jad.2026.122586, PMID 42833407.** Wroclaw Medical University psychiatry. PubMed-verified.

ChatGPT, DeepSeek and Replika across three suicidal-risk scenarios escalating over seven days,
C-SSRS-informed. Overall human referral 85.7% / 76.2% / 9.5%. Day-7 imminent-risk referral 9/9, 4/9,
1/9. And the finding that matters for us: **deliberate bypass succeeded in 6/9, 7/9 and 8/9
attempts** — the majority of attempts against every system tested, including the one with the best
unprompted referral rate.

### The gap this exposes → EDIT 1

Our exploratory endpoint is "**Crisis-safeguard activation consistency across matched suicidality
presentations**," ascertained per Table A4 by "safeguard-language detection on matched suicidality
presentations, physician-adjudicated."

That measures whether the safeguard **fires**. It does not measure whether the safeguard **holds**.

A system that activates its safeguard reliably and can then be talked out of it in two-thirds of
attempts would score as having consistent crisis-safeguard activation. That would be true, and
misleading. Suicidality is the highest-harm domain the protocol touches, which makes it the worst
place to leave the activation/robustness distinction implicit.

**EDIT 1 costs nothing.** No adversarial prompting, no jailbreak probes, no scenario variants, no
participant burden, **no effect on the §8.4 power calculation.** It is a §10 boundary stating what an
activation measurement does and does not establish, plus an optional one-clause companion in
Table A4 where the endpoint is defined.

**Transfer limits written into the proposed text.** 27 simulated trajectories against three consumer
systems, two of which this protocol does not test — and Replika, which carries the worst numbers, is
a companion app rather than a triage system. The text cites bypass as an established property of the
class requiring disclosure, not as a rate estimate for our systems, and reproduces the authors' own
generalizability caveat.

## Put to the PI as an option — deliberately NOT proposed

An actual robustness probe (a pre-specified bypass attempt on scenarios where a safeguard fired).
I am not proposing it, for a reason beyond cost: **it is an ethics question before it is a methods
question.** It would generate transcripts in which a system is talked out of protecting a simulated
suicidal user, and §6 would have to address handling, storage, and whether any of it reaches lay
Arm C participants. That is not a methods tweak to slip into a patch.

It also expands §8.4 power, and it is now the **third** multi-turn probe contending for the same
budget alongside Arm C1 (`[DECISION 4]`) and the 17 September stability probe. Recommendation:
decide multi-turn probing **once across all three**, and route the adversarial variant through the
ethics question first.

## Why almost nothing else is proposed, despite a ten-reference week

**Nine of the ten new references are preprints**, and none is cited in any proposed edit. Two
deserve the PI's eye anyway:

- **"LLMs Anchor on Chief Complaint and Fail to Integrate Evidence in Sequential Clinical Triage"**
  (arXiv) — labels anchored on early chief-complaint exchanges at every checkpoint, and **prompting
  interventions did not lift the plateau**; expert clinicians QWK 0.887–0.929 against 0.295 for the
  best model. This sits **in tension with BiasMedQA**, where debiasing and few-shot prompting *did*
  significantly reduce bias — a claim the pending 10 September EDIT 2 relies on. If this survives
  peer review it complicates that text. Flagged to watch; not acted on.
- **"The complexities of patient-centred conversational artificial intelligence"** (arXiv) —
  2,053 real patient-chatbot conversations and 1,164 clinician-graded cases finding that
  **communication style significantly alters triage outcomes**, with systems evaluated only on
  articulate simulated patients risking amplified disparities. This is the closest match to our
  dual-register premise yet seen. **It has no DOI and no PMID and is websearch-verified only —
  quarantined, cited nowhere.**

And one item is **out of scope by design**, which is worth stating rather than leaving as an
apparent omission: the UCLA **"Triage and Referral Behavior Across Patient-Facing Medical AI
Products"** preprint found branded health-AI products over-triaging low-acuity cases (28% vs 3% vs
2%) and steering toward **affiliated, fee-requiring services**. That is a commercial self-referral
failure mode. §5.5 Arm A tests "each frontier model" — general-purpose systems with no affiliated
services to steer toward, so the mechanism has no referent in our design. Adding a
referral-destination endpoint would measure an axis the systems under test cannot exhibit. Strong
successor-study hypothesis; not an edit.

Also screened and not proposed: an EU psychiatric-AI liability viewpoint (*JMIR*, PMID 42832687,
peer-reviewed) — §11 already cites the AI Act, MDR and GDPR and has a revision pending from
