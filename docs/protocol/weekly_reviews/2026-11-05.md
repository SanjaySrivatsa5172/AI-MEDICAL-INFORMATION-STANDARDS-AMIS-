## The proposal: the protocol has no equity sentence, and now the field has named that

**Alamoudi A, Gai Y, Zghebi SS, Kontopantelis E, Brown BC. "Patient safety and health equity of
AI-enabled triage tools in primary care: a systematic review." *BMJ Health Care Inform*
2026;33(1). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmjhci-2026-102427), PMID 42951327.** PROSPERO
CRD420251179046. University of Manchester. According to PubMed, peer-reviewed and verified today.

This record has sat in the library since 29 October **with a blank author field**, because PubMed
then carried neither abstract nor author list — it was screened on confirmed title and venue alone
and listed as a standing retrieval request. Both are now indexed. This is the first cycle in which
the paper could be read, and it is the most consequential item of the week.

Searching four databases from January 2015 to November 2025 for prospective, real-world evaluations
of AI triage tools in primary care, it found:

1. Four eligible studies, **all of them rule-based or decision-tree systems. No machine-learning or
   generative-AI system met the eligibility criteria at all.**
2. Safe triage 81–97.6%, under-triage 2–17.2%, over-triage 8–29%. The pooled safe-triage proportion
   across three studies (n = 1303) was 0.908, 95% CI **0.319 to 0.995**, I² = 97.0% — which the
   authors state is hypothesis-generating rather than a summary measure. We should quote that
   caution and never the point estimate.
3. **No included study analysed differential triage accuracy by any sociodemographic factor**,
   despite holding the demographic data. The authors name this a systemic evidence gap and propose a
   Safety-Equity Evidence Mapping matrix.
4. Safety outcomes throughout were "confined to technical performance proxies rather than direct
   measures of harm."

Finding (1) is strong external support for the protocol's premise, and it is better than anything we
have: a peer-reviewed systematic review establishing that the generative-AI evidence base this study
addresses is empty, rather than our asserting it. That belongs in the anticipated-questions brief.
It does **not** require a protocol edit — §1 makes no novelty claim that currently lacks support.

Finding (3) is the one that touches the text. **The protocol contains no equity, sociodemographic,
subgroup, health-literacy or language-access term anywhere in its 33 pages** — grepped this cycle,
zero hits. §10 opens by saying its boundaries are "stated here rather than left for reviewers to
find"; by its own standard the section is incomplete. The proposal adds a fourth boundary saying
plainly that the design carries no sociodemographic variable, that the dual register is a wording
contrast and not a proxy for any patient group, and that closing the gap needs a population-sampled
successor study. It adds no stratification, no scenario authoring, no analysis, and makes no power
claim. Full text and application cases in `proposed_edits_2026-11-05.patch`.

## The sync nearly manufactured a duplicate, and the reconciler told me why

The identifier-set diff reported two "ours-only" records. One was genuine — the Sheng B *NEJM AI*
viewpoint, PI-supplied, carried over every week. The other was a trap.

Our library held the AMIE ambulatory-primary-care feasibility study as an **arXiv preprint**
(`10.48550/arXiv.2603.08448`). Canonical no longer holds that DOI anywhere. It holds instead
*"Conversational diagnostic artificial intelligence in ambulatory primary care: a prospective
feasibility study"* — the published *Lancet* version, PMID 42849491, DOI
[10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01535-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(26)01535-7), same `date_added`.
**Canonical upgraded the record in place rather than adding one.**

A sync that treated our preprint as a legitimate ours-only record and preserved it would have
produced a preprint/published pair for one study — precisely the Jia problem I have reported every
week since 1 October. The sync dropped it. The anchor set is written down: our arXiv DOI must occur
exactly once in ours, must be **absent** from canonical, and the Lancet replacement must occur
exactly once in canonical, or the script exits non-zero. Result: canonical 230 + Sheng = **231**, no
repeated PMID or DOI, 14-key schema clean on all 231.

The reconciler's own provenance note for today states the policy outright:

> "…it is the peer-reviewed version of the arXiv 2603.08448 preprint already in the library, so that
> existing entry was **UPGRADED IN PLACE** to the published *Lancet* article (per the
> one-entry-per-study dedup policy; cf the 2026-08-07 preprint-dup precedent)."

It also records that the 4 November fresh-session scan *did* create the duplicate and that the
reconciler collapsed it, 231 → 230.

**This sharpens the standing Jia recommendation into something much more actionable.** For five
weeks I have been recommending that canonical deduplicate the Jia et al. OpenEvidence
preprint/published pair. The implicit reading was that the reconciler lacked a supersession policy.
That reading was wrong. The reconciler has the policy, has a precedent dated 2026-08-07, applied it
correctly to AMIE today, and catches scan-generated duplicates on sight. **Jia specifically escaped

over the Jia pair" — a single back-fill, not a process change. This matters because that study drove
all three PI-approved edits of 3 September and is one of only two anchoring nulls in the §0

   already states the Fraile Navarro format critique and answers it by construction, and §4.1
   already specifies the deterministic phrase inventory "applied to the model's own free-text
   output" with no language model judging the primary endpoint. The new preprint corroborates a
   design choice already made and justified. Nothing to add.
2. **Could it disturb the power calculation?** No, and it is worth stating why the question is
   closed: §8.4 is UNRESOLVED/BLOCKING and states that no power figures may be stated at this
   revision, while the acceptance machinery in §7.2.1 and §7.3.3 is built on one-sided
   cluster-bootstrap confidence bounds rather than point estimates, each saying in terms that it
   therefore states no sample size and makes no power claim while §8.4 stays open. The
   protocol asserts no baseline CMR, so no result about baseline CMR can invalidate it. That is the
   bound-based construction doing exactly the work it was put there for.
3. **Does §13's stated library count need refreshing?** Yes, and it is still queued, not applied.
   §13 reads "178 entries as of 3 September 2026" against a live figure of **231**. The correction
