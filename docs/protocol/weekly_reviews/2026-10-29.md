## The result the PI will be asked about at IRB

**Nori H, Daswani M, Kelly C, … Suleyman M, King D, Horvitz E. "Sequential diagnosis with language
models." *Science* 2026;394(6822):423–428. doi 10.1126/science.aeh7930, PMID 42912144.** Microsoft.
PubMed-verified.

SDBench: 304 NEJM clinicopathological-conference cases plus **325 MIMIC-IV-ED emergency-department
admissions**, each converted into an interactive stepwise encounter. An orchestrator (MAI-DxO)
simulating a physician panel reached **83.2% on CPC cases and 80.3% on the ED cohort, against 36.1%
and 46.8% for generalist physicians.**

This is the most quotable "AI outperforms physicians" result published to date, it is in *Science*,
and it includes an emergency-department cohort. **It falsifies nothing here**, and the reasons should
be ready rather than improvised:

1. **The authors bound it themselves, in the abstract**: "Although these findings were obtained in a
   controlled benchmark setting…"
2. **The task is sequential diagnosis with cost-aware test ordering, not triage disposition.** The
   question is "what is the diagnosis," not "does this person need an emergency department now."
   Our primary endpoint is a disposition failure under asymmetric harm.
3. **The input is a clinical presentation, not a patient's everyday language.** There is no lay
   register anywhere in the design — the protocol's distinguishing axis is absent.
4. **The comparator is generalist physicians on NEJM CPC puzzles**, a deliberately hard diagnostic
   genre. Arm B here is emergency physicians answering triage questions inside their own specialty.
5. **The system is an orchestrated multi-agent scaffold, not a consumer chatbot.** This protocol
   evaluates what a frightened person can open on a phone.

Taken together: a strong result about a different task, a different input register, a different
comparator and a different artefact. Worth stating plainly in the anticipated-questions brief
alongside the Tiryaki result recorded on 15 October — these will be raised together.

## Corroboration of the central premise, in our own register

**Counihan CM, Duncan RJD, Bullock MG, Mallina R. "Diagnostic and Triage Performance of Large
Language Models in Hand and Wrist Complaints: A Standardised Vignette Evaluation." *Hand (N Y)*
2026. doi 10.1177/15589447261497439, PMID 42948859.** PubMed-verified.

Fifty **first-person "patient-voice" vignettes** — our lay register — across three free consumer
LLMs. The numbers are the protocol's thesis restated by someone else:

- Overall triage accuracy 83.3%, with **15.3% under-triage against 1.3% over-triage** — a ratio of
  roughly 12 to 1, in the harmful direction.
- **Under-triage rose monotonically with acuity: 0.0% minor → 16.7% elective → 21.7% trauma
  (P = .0348).**
- Diagnostic accuracy 100% minor / 96.7% elective / **65.0% trauma** (P < .001).
- Authors' conclusion: "Given the potential for false reassurance and delayed care in time-critical
  injuries, LLM outputs should not be relied upon for triage decisions."

Why this drives no edit: it corroborates the asymmetric-harm premise and the severity-weighting
scheme the protocol already encodes (SW 1–3, the SW = 3 atypical-catastrophe class, under- and
over-triage reported at each ESI boundary). Corroboration of a standing design choice is not a reason
to edit. It is, however, the best available external evidence that the protocol is measuring the
right thing in the right register, and it belongs in the Scientific Introduction if the PI ever
revisits it.

## A second demonstration that under-triage is structured, not scattered

Last week's Çalışkan paper found undertriage clustered by **age and mechanism**. Counihan now finds it
clustered by **acuity**, monotonically. Two peer-reviewed papers in two weeks, in different
specialties, both finding that under-triage concentrates rather than scatters.

This strengthens the flag I raised on 22 October rather than creating a new one: **the protocol
pre-specifies no patient-characteristic or presentation-type subgroup analysis**, so an aggregate
Critical Miss Rate can conceal exactly this structure. I am still not proposing an edit, for the same
reason as last week — whether the 50-scenario set can power a stratified analysis is a design-and-
power question, and pre-specifying an unpowerable analysis would be worse than stating none.
**With two independent demonstrations now on the record, I would raise this as a substantive item
when §8.4 is addressed, not as drafting.**

## Two checks that came out clean — recorded because they were run

- **Is the register hypothesis directionally presupposed?** The literature is now genuinely mixed:
  Albalawi et al. (*Diagnostics*, PMID 42938107) found that adding narrative clinical dialogues to
  structured data cut under-triage by **79.7% relative** (91.9% in high-acuity cases) by surfacing
  red-flag symptoms absent from structured fields — narrative *helping*; Fraile Navarro (standing
  alert #2) found naturalistic phrasing improved accuracy 6.4pp; Samsel found narratives scoring
  lower, confounded with answer format. If the protocol's register hypothesis presupposed that lay
  language is simply worse, a result in the other direction would be awkward to report.
  **It does not.** §2 objective 2 reads "to **quantify the effect** of presentation register (lay
  narrative vs expert vignette) on triage safety" — neutral and two-sided. The only directional
  colouring is the word "vulnerability" in §8.1's interaction sentence, and an interaction term is
  inherently two-sided, so the statistical machinery is sound. No edit: a one-word framing nuance in
  a sentence whose anchor the 24 September patch already claims is not worth a seventh patch.
- **Is CMR's case-mix dependence stated?** The protocol does not state the acuity distribution of the
  50 scenarios, and Counihan shows under-triage varies with acuity, so the CMR is case-mix dependent.
  But §5.2 makes the enrichment intent explicit (severity weights, the SW = 3 atypical-catastrophe
  class, the gold disposition scale), and §10 already carries "a benchmark result is a measurement,
  not a clearance." The gap is at most an implied-but-unstated note, and §10 is already contended by
  two queued patches. Recorded, not proposed.

  a systematic review." *BMJ Health Care Inform* 2026;33(1). doi 10.1136/bmjhci-2026-102427,
  PMID 42951327. **No abstract available — not retrieved**; nothing quoted. By title and keywords it

  the sole altered variable; all three diverged. Joins the fairness cluster.

  error (premature necrosectomy advocacy) in the Gemini arm, 3.7%.
- **"When Empathy Outpaces Accuracy"** (*NEJM AI* letter, PMID 42932625) — makes this protocol's
  severity-weighting argument directly: rater-identified errors involved reassurance about symptoms
  that would prompt assessment for alcohol withdrawal, and the original framework did not weight
  errors by clinical severity. Good supporting citation for the Failure Severity Index rationale.
- **"Generative AI for Establishing the Chief Complaint"** (*J Clin Med* viewpoint, Sorin & Klang,
  PMID 42941427) — proposes measuring the proportion of patients whose concerns are missing after
  AI-assisted intake, "including whether concerns requiring urgent assessment were missed." Close
  cousin of our interaction-failure metrics (information omission).
- Dental-emergency multilingual chatbot audit (PMID 42945123), GraphRAG postoperative QA with a
  75.0% emergency-intent accuracy arm (PMID 42938952), and a multi-agent psychiatric-screening
  framework with a 2,000-sentence suicidal-ideation red-flag benchmark (PMID 42946723) — all
  ancillary.
