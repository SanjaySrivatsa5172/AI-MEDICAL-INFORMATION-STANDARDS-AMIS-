## The §7.3 result, and why it changes nothing

**Carvalho E Silva M, Carvalho E Silva F, de Paula Gomes Nunes de Castro R, Gonçalves A, Figueiredo
Delgado A. "Large language models are unstable as autonomous medical evaluators across clinical
tasks." *J Am Med Inform Assoc* 2026. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag190), PMID 42995166.**
Universidade de São Paulo. According to PubMed, peer-reviewed; verified today via PubMed and Scite.

Ten LLMs (3 local open-source, 7 API — Claude, GPT, DeepSeek) used as autonomous judges on three
tasks with human reference labels: LiveQA-Med answer quality (n = 300), PubMedQA appraisal
(n = 300), and **ESI triage on MIMIC-IV-Ext (n = 256)**. Three scorings per model per case at
temperature zero — **25,680 judgments**. Cluster-bootstrap intervals, quadratic-weighted Cohen's κ,
FDR correction.

Three findings:

1. **Evaluator performance is unstable across tasks.** No model ranked top-3 on all three; every
   model shifted ≥3 rank positions between at least one pair of tasks.
2. **ESI level 1 (Resuscitation) cases were under-triaged in 89.1% of evaluations, in all 10
   models** (range 70.2%–96.9%).
3. **Best agreement only reached the human inter-rater range** — Claude Opus quadratic-weighted
   κ = 0.60 [0.54, 0.66].

The authors conclude that performance validated on one task should not be assumed to transfer, and
that "emergency triage demands human oversight at the highest-acuity tier."

**This is strong external corroboration of §7.3 and §7.6, not a challenge to them.** Finding (3) is a
second empirical instance of the argument that drove the whole jury amendment — a judge can sit at
the human inter-rater range and still be unusable — alongside the MedHELM ICC = 0.47 case already on
the record. Finding (2) is the tail-concentration failure the amendment was built around, now
measured. And the paper's methods converge independently on ours: cluster-bootstrap intervals,
quadratic-weighted agreement, temperature zero, ≥3 runs per item, version-pinned models.

**The check I ran, and why I am proposing nothing.** Findings (1) and (2) together suggest an obvious
edit: stratify a gate by severity weight, so a jury cannot be promoted on a pooled conditional
false-credit rate while failing on SW = 3. Gate 3 is directional but **pooled**; the severity
stratification in §7.3.5 sits in reported statistics, which that section states are explicitly not
gates. So the gap is real in the arithmetic.

It is not a gap in the protocol, because **the PI already decided it.** The `[RESOLVED]` note closing
§7.3 says, verbatim:

> "tail concentration is addressed by shadow-mode measurement and by JDDR rather than by **stratified
> gating, which is not estimable at this design's stratum sizes**"

That was approved on 29 August. The rejection rests on **estimability at our stratum sizes** — not on
whether tail concentration matters. The JAMIA paper strengthens the case that it matters; it says
nothing about our stratum sizes and so leaves the grounds of the decision untouched. The amendment
documents sixteen rejected proposals specifically so they are not re-litigated, and this belongs in
that spirit. **Recorded, not proposed.**

What the paper *is* good for: it is a peer-reviewed JAMIA conclusion that emergency triage demands
human oversight at the highest-acuity tier — third-party endorsement of the physician-sovereign
architecture, and the cleanest answer available to the objection §7.6 names as the one the study's
own thesis invites. It belongs in the anticipated-questions brief.

## A real-world disposition study the scan has never seen

**Saenz AD, Schumacher E, Naik D. "From Concept to Clinic: Real World Evidence for Autonomous AI
Deployment in Primary Care Telemedicine." medRxiv **preprint**,
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.03.18.26348749).** Verified through Scite — exists, no editorial
notices, not predatory. **Not peer-reviewed. Not in PubMed** (searched today, zero hits), so no
published version is indexed.

A multiagent LLM system inside a nationwide US primary-care telemedicine platform, evaluated against
treating clinicians who were blinded to its output, across **2,379 real patient encounters**. Top-1
diagnosis matched the clinician in 91.3% overall, 96.3% above a pre-specified safety-confidence
threshold. **Disposition error rate 2.5% overall, with "no errors in suggestions to emergency room or
home management."** The authors argue system architecture rather than model capability is what makes
autonomous deployment safe, and propose a staged, task-calibrated framework for deploying AI
autonomously.

**This is the most quotable challenge to the protocol's motivating direction yet recorded** — more
pointed than the *Science* SDBench result of 29 October, because it is real patients, disposition
rather than diagnosis, and emergency referral specifically. It will be raised. The answer should be
prepared, not improvised, and it is sound:

1. **It is a preprint**, and the protocol's citation policy labels preprints as such.
2. **A licensed clinician was in the loop on every encounter** — patients "completed full visits with
   licensed clinicians," and the AI's output was compared with the clinician's, never substituted
   for it. That is the S2 configuration. Our subject systems run in the S4 configuration with no
   clinician anywhere, which is the whole point of the Sheng classification in §1.
3. **It is an intake system inside a telemedicine platform**, reached by someone who has already
   decided to seek care. The protocol measures what a frightened person gets from a consumer app
   *before* that decision — the register and the population are both different.
4. **"No errors in suggestions to emergency room" is a numerator, not a rate.** The abstract does not
   give the emergency-referral denominator, and I did not retrieve it. A zero over a small
   denominator is not a zero rate, and it should not be quoted as one until the denominator is in
   hand. **Flagged as unretrieved.**
5. Its own thesis — that architecture and integrated safety mechanisms, not model capability, make
   deployment safe — is an argument *for* evaluating deployed consumer systems as artefacts, which
   is what this protocol does.

**I have not added it to the library.** Curation is the reconciler's remit and adding it our side
would fork us from the canonical lineage, for the same reason as the Jia duplicate and the two
standing misses. It is passed along below.
