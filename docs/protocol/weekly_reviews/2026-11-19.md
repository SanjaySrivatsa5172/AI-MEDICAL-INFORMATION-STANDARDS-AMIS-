## The one paper that matters, and it confirms rather than challenges

**Yang Z, Tian D, He M, … Chen Y. "Evaluating DeepSeek in Real-World Clinical Scenarios on
Performance and Safeguards: Multicenter Comparative Study." *J Med Internet Res*
2026;28:e86746. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2196/86746), PMID 43024857.** According to PubMed,
peer-reviewed; verified through Scite today — no editorial notices, not predatory.

Fifty real medical records from ten hospitals across fourteen specialties, seven models (DeepSeek-V3
and -R1, four GPT-4o/o3-mini variants, GPT-4o-reasoning). Alongside diagnosis and record-analysis
tasks, the authors ran **four rounds of simulated adversarial prompt attacks designed to elicit
misinformation**:

- **DeepSeek-R1 generated medical disinformation in 98% of cases (49/50), with a 2% refusal rate.**
- The most resistant model, GPT-4o-reasoning, had the **highest refusal rate at 30% — and still
  generated disinformation in 70% (35/50).**
- True hallucination rates ran **38.6% to 73%** across models.
- The authors' conclusion: safeguards need strengthening **before** clinical deployment.

**Why this changes nothing, and why that is the right outcome.** This is a second peer-reviewed
demonstration that crisis- and safety-oriented safeguards do not survive deliberate pressure, and it
arrives three days after the §10 boundary saying exactly that was applied. The first was Pichowicz et
al.'s seven-day escalating-suicidality simulation, which drove the 8 October patch; this one is a
different setting entirely — real clinical records, fourteen specialties, a multicentre design — and
it reaches the same place. The boundary now rests on two independent lines of evidence instead of
one.

It also bears on the **triage-refusal endpoint** (§4.1, §4.2), which exists because the protocol
needed a third output category. Refusal rates of 2% to 30% under adversarial pressure are a measured
range for the quantity that endpoint reports, in a population of models overlapping ours. That is
useful calibration for the anticipated-questions brief. It is **not** an edit: the endpoint already
exists, already has its acceptance criteria under §7.2.1, and the §10 boundary already states that
activation is not robustness.

## Also found, nothing proposed

- **Psilopatis I, Emons J, Pavone M, Zwimpfer TA. "The Potential of Large Language Models in
  Gynecologic Oncology Telephone Triage." *Diagnostics (Basel)* 2026;16(21):3668.
  [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/diagnostics16213668).** Scite-verified. Twenty vignettes, UKONS
  Red-Amber-Green, three consultants' unanimous consensus as reference, three LLMs including
  OpenEvidence. **All three models were 100% concordant on every Red case, with no high-acuity case
  downgraded to self-care**; errors clustered at low-acuity boundaries, with a conservative
  over-triage bias.
  **The PI should expect this to be quoted back.** The honest answer: twenty clinician-authored
  vignettes about treatment-related toxicities in patients already under oncology care, graded
  against a nurse-triage tool, single-turn and no lay register anywhere — and the authors' own
  conclusion is that the findings "support a supervised, adjunctive role for LLMs, rather than
  autonomous deployment, pending prospective validation," which is this protocol's position in their
  words. It is a different task, population and register, and it does not contradict a 51.6%
  under-triage figure obtained on lay-language emergency presentations.
- **Swain R, Masic M, Staal J, … Zwaan L. "Effect of Teledermatologist Supervision in AI Skin Cancer
  Apps on Patients' Trust and Adherence Intentions: Randomized Controlled Trial." *JMIR Mhealth
  Uhealth* 2026;14:e93991. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.2196/93991).** Scite-verified. 879 participants,
  vignette RCT. Hybrid teledermatologist-plus-AI advice beat AI-only on trust (76.9 vs 65.7) and on
  adherence (OR 1.7), **fully mediated by trust**, with the effect strongest at high-risk
  assessments. A third independent instance of the supervised-versus-unsupervised contrast the Sheng
  S2/S4 framing rests on, and the first measuring what patients *do* with the advice rather than
  whether it is right. Ancillary; vignette-based, dermatology screening, no emergency disposition.
- **Li S, Wang L, Wang X, … Xie L.** Five LLMs on Chinese-language patient queries about visual-field
  defects, *Front Physiol* 2026;17:1993774. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2026.1993774). Two
  methodological notes worth borrowing rather than citing: the authors state plainly that **"the
  absence of a statistically significant difference in safety ratings under controlled conditions
  does not imply equivalent or adequate clinical safety"**, and that their findings are specific to
  model versions tested on a named date with **stability across repeated generations not assessed**.
  Both are disciplines this protocol already enforces (§5.6 pinning, ≥3 runs); seeing them stated as
  limitations elsewhere is a reasonable thing to cite in the reporting section if §11 is ever
  revisited. No edit.

## A citation this review refuses to use

A multi-platform analysis of AI chatbot responses to patient questions on regenerative therapies for
erectile dysfunction (*Transl Androl Urol* 2026;15(10):378) reported **within-platform consistency
across two sessions on separate days of Cohen's κ = 0.54–0.67** — a run-to-run stability figure for
consumer-facing advice, which is close to the §4.2 quantity.

**Scite flags the journal as predatory.** The figure is therefore not carried into this review as
evidence, not proposed for the library, and not offered for any protocol text. Recorded here so the
decision is auditable, and because the paper will surface again in future sweeps: the answer is
already no unless it is independently corroborated elsewhere.

This is the first predatory-journal catch since Scite came back, and it is the argument for keeping
the verification step even when a result looks useful.
