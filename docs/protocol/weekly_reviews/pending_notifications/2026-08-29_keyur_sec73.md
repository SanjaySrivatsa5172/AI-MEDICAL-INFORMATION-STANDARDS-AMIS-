WHAT WAS WRONG WITH THE OLD SECTION

Two acceptance gates, both asking the same question — how often does the jury agree with
physicians — and that question fails in three ways.

1. Agreement counts the easy cases. When the responses physicians judge deficient are a
   minority, a jury can miss every one and still post high agreement. Under conditions
   this study expects, a jury reproducing NONE of the physicians' deficiency findings
   returns Gwet AC1 = 0.947 against a gate set at 0.75.
2. Agreement is direction-blind. This study exists because missing a heart attack and
   over-calling one are not the same mistake (under 5% undertriage against up to 35%
   overtriage). Every symmetric agreement statistic — AC1, kappa, Krippendorff's alpha —
   counts both identically. A dangerously lenient jury and a merely fussy one produce
   numbers identical to four decimal places.
3. Agreement is an average and the failure is not. If the jury does well on ordinary
   cases and badly on atypical catastrophes and benign-looking presentations — the cases
   the benchmark exists to probe — the average conceals it.

Underneath all three: "majority verdict" recorded a 2-1 split with the same confidence as
a 3-0. On a 2-1 split, three judges are worth one judge.

Supporting evidence, verified: MedHELM (Bedi et al., Nat Med 2026, PMID 41559415) cleared
exactly our old Gate 1 — LLM-jury ICC 0.47 against clinicians, "notably outperforming" the
0.43 between clinicians themselves — while Koo & Li classify anything below 0.50 as poor.
Beating human disagreement is necessary and demonstrably not sufficient. Ho et al.
(BMC Med Educ 2026, PMID 42387499) show AC1 = 0.976 alongside Fleiss kappa = -0.01 on
identical ratings.

WHAT THE NEW SECTION DOES (7.3.1 through 7.3.6)

- Panel fixed at EXACTLY three frontier judges. No judge may share a vendor OR MODEL
  FAMILY with the system it is scoring — judge affinity is a similarity effect, not a
  self-identity one. Position randomised per item. Each judge run at least three times,
  within-judge majority as its verdict.
- The rating scale is now stated per component, and the coefficient follows the scale
  (AC1 nominal/binary, AC2 with linear weighting for ordinal). A threshold on a
  coefficient whose scale is unstated is not well defined.
- A verdict requires UNANIMITY. The full per-judge vote vector and a jury_margin field are
  stored. 2-1 and no-mode items are "jury-indeterminate" and route to physician
  adjudication within a pre-registered budget, ordered by hazard (SW=3 atypical
  catastrophes first).
- THREE conjunctive gates, each on a one-sided cluster-bootstrap confidence bound, never
  a point estimate: relative (repaired so both sides are computed at the same aggregation
  level, plus a Delphi-label anchor where one exists), absolute floor, and a new
  DIRECTIONAL gate — the conditional false-credit rate, i.e. of the components physicians
  rate deficient, the proportion the jury rates adequate. That third gate is what finally
  matches the study's asymmetric loss function.
- AC1 is DEMOTED from gate to reported statistic, published alongside kappa, the observed
  prevalence, and the raw per-cell counts.
- New diagnostics: Jury-Determinism Dissociation Rate (the only check that can catch judge
  error running in a direction physicians would share), double-fault rate conditioned on
  the gold label, per-judge self-preference by family, register paired contrast.
- Shadow mode: when a component is not promoted the jury still runs, reported as an
  exploratory finding at zero scoring weight. Non-promotion is the DEFAULT state.

EFFECT ON DECISION 2 — the PI should know this one changed
DECISION 2 is now split. 2a is the threshold-setting RULE (pre-registered mapping on the
blinded validation set, evaluated on confidence bounds) and closes at filing at the level
of form. 2b records the agreement band as a reported diagnostic, not a gate. The old
"Gwet AC1 >= 0.75" threshold is struck through in the §0 register and marked retired, with
the reason.
