EDIT 1 — Cite a contrasting anchoring null alongside Sheppert
Sections affected: Scientific Introduction (anchoring prediction; Key Reference 22);
PROTOCOL_v3 section 0, DECISION 1 evidence note

What changed: the Scientific Introduction now cites, directly after the Sheppert
anchoring figure, a peer-reviewed result that points the other way — and the DECISION 1
evidence note is tempered accordingly.

Why: Jaarsma EH, Ring D, Wickman J, Drost A. "Can Large Language Models Preserve
Diagnostic Accuracy Despite Patient Self-Diagnosis and Framing Bias in Hypothetical
Upper-Extremity Scenarios?" J Hand Surg Am 2026. doi 10.1016/j.jhsa.2026.07.014,
PMID 42640232. ChatGPT (GPT-5) across 180 randomized vignettes in five benign
upper-extremity conditions: 92% diagnostic accuracy, unaffected by the patient's stated
self-diagnosis; the model deviated from the proposed self-diagnosis in 65% of scenarios,
89% of those deviations landing on the correct intended diagnosis. The authors conclude
the model showed "limited susceptibility to the anchoring, confirmation, and acquiescence
biases known to affect human diagnostic reasoning."

This is a genuine contrasting null to the Sheppert finding our introduction leans on
(LLMs anchoring roughly 4x more than physicians). It falsifies nothing — different model
generation, benign non-emergency conditions, diagnosis selection rather than disposition,
and no asymmetric-harm axis — but leaving it uncited would have handed a reviewer an easy
objection. The same standard was applied to the Ada et al. register null in the 21 August
cycle: competing nulls are declared, not omitted. The framing added is that anchoring
susceptibility may be model-generation- and stakes-dependent, and that no study has yet
shown such resistance where the missed diagnosis is catastrophic — which is precisely the
regime this benchmark measures.

Effect on DECISION 1 (adopt or defer Change 12): the case is TEMPERED, NOT REVERSED. The
evidence note now records that anchoring resistance has been demonstrated only for a
newest-generation model, on benign conditions, with no disposition decision and no
asymmetric-harm axis — and that Change 12 is the instrument that would test whether it
holds where missing is catastrophic.
