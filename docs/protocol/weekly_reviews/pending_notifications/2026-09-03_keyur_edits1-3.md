EDIT 1 — TRIAGE-REFUSAL IS NOW A THIRD OUTPUT CATEGORY (the important one)
Their platform declined to assign any triage level in 65 of 960 responses (6.8%), exclusively
on symptom-only non-urgent prompts, and the authors recommend treating refusal as a distinct
category.

Why that is our problem: §4.1 ascertains Critical Miss Rate from escalation phrases in the
model's own text. A response saying "I can't determine urgency, please consult a clinician"
contains no escalation phrase — so our classifier scored it as a CRITICAL MISS, identically to
false reassurance. Those are not clinically equivalent. False reassurance discourages
care-seeking; a refusal that routes the patient to a clinician is weak but not harmful. We were
collapsing them, biasing the primary endpoint by an unknown amount in an unknown direction.

Now: refusals are recorded as triage-refusal and EXCLUDED from the CMR numerator and
denominator; the per-model refusal rate is a secondary endpoint; and the primary endpoint is
additionally reported under a pre-specified sensitivity analysis that counts refusals as misses.
§7.2.1's acceptance criteria extend to refusal detection as a three-way classification
(escalation present / absent / no disposition), and Table A4 has a new row.

This defect existed regardless of whether their result generalises. It is the kind of thing the
weekly review exists to catch.

EDIT 2 — A SECOND ANCHORING NULL, THIS ONE BENCHMARK-MATCHED
Anchoring statements did not alter recommendations: OR 1.08, 95% CI 0.62–1.88, Holm-adjusted
p = 1.0. That is the second null in eight days (after Jaarsma, PMID 42640232). The §0 DECISION 1
evidence note now records both.

The bound is what keeps Change 12 alive, and it is the same bound for both nulls: neither comes
from a consumer patient-facing system reasoning WITHOUT objective clinical data. OpenEvidence is
physician-facing and retrieval-augmented.

EDIT 3 — THE INFORMATIONAL REGIME GOVERNS TRIAGE SAFETY
Same paper: supplying objective clinical data ELIMINATED emergency under-triage (25% → 0%,
p = 0.005) and cut non-urgent over-triage (78.7% → 57.8%, p = 0.014). That is the cleanest
published support for the premise behind our register factor — a patient typing symptoms into a
consumer assistant supplies neither objective data nor clinical structure. Cited in the
Scientific Introduction, added as Key Reference 23.

Their under-triage was 12.5% against ChatGPT Health's 51.6% on the same instrument. The
deployment-context difference is the whole explanation, and stating it that way strengthens our
protocol rather than weakening it.
