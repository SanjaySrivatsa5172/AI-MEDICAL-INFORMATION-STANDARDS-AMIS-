## The week's finding: the register factor rests on an unmeasured assumption

**Çalışkan YK, Başak F, Erdem O. "When algorithms triage trauma: Diagnostic accuracy, undertriage
risk, and prompt fragility in frontier large language models." *Surgery* 2026;196:110319.
doi 10.1016/j.surg.2026.110319, PMID 42214316.** 150 trauma vignettes, three frontier families,
locked prompt, surgeon-consensus reference standard anchored on Injury Severity Score.

Aggregate performance was strong — sensitivity 89.4%, specificity 86.5%, pooled κ 0.74. The useful
part is underneath it:

> "Across standardized lexical perturbations applied to all 150 vignettes, **11.2% of repeated
> prompts changed triage category, with a maximum instability of 24.5%.**"

### Why that lands on §5.2

§5.2 declares the dual register "a deliberate distribution-shift probe, **not a stylistic variant**."
That is an assertion about what the contrast measures. Grepping the protocol for perturbation,
paraphrase, lexical, wording, rephrasing or invariance returns **nothing** — there is no paraphrase
control anywhere in the document.

So if models flip triage category at 11.2% on rewording that holds clinical content fixed, and we
observe a lay-versus-expert difference of comparable size, **the register effect is not identifiable
against a floor the study has not measured.** The register factor is this protocol's distinctive
claim — the thing that separates it from the clinician-facing triage benchmarks now appearing
monthly — and it is currently resting on an assumption stated in print.

Three distinct quantities, and none substitutes for another:

| Quantity | Varies | Already in protocol |
|---|---|---|
| Run-to-run instability (§4.2) | nothing — identical prompt repeated | yes |
| Any-run critical-miss rate (24 Sep proposal) | nothing; fixes tail-blindness of aggregation | proposed |
| **Paraphrase-invariance floor** | **wording, content held fixed** | **absent** |

**EDIT 1 costs nothing** — no measurement, scenario, model call or participant burden, and **no
effect on the §8.4 power calculation.** It states the interpretive constraint and narrows the §5.2
claim so it rests on *how the two forms are constructed* (information order, completeness, disclosure
of vitals, clinical vocabulary — not synonym choice) rather than on a measurement the study does not
make. Transfer limits are in the text: Çalışkan is trauma secondary triage under a locked prompt, so
the figure shows a floor exists and is non-trivial, not what ours would be.

## The option I would actually argue for

A within-register paraphrase condition on a scenario subset — same clinical content, same information
order, wording varied — reported as the floor. Of the four expansions surfaced since 17 September,
**this is the one I would recommend if the PI adopts exactly one**, and the reasoning is not that it
is cheapest:

- **No human-subjects cost at all** — no new participants, no new panels, no adversarial prompting,
  no ethics question (unlike the 8 Oct safeguard probe).
- **No new clinical content to adjudicate.** The gold label is unchanged because the clinical facts
  are unchanged; the Delphi panel does not reconvene.
- **A floor is a nuisance parameter, not an endpoint**, so it needs far less precision than the
  primary and can sit on a small subset.
- **It defends a claim already in print.** Without it, a reviewer can ask whether the register effect
  is wording noise, and the honest answer today is that the study cannot say.

Costs stated plainly: it does expand §8.4, which is the blocking item; content-preserving paraphrases
are a skilled authoring task and **must not be delegated to a model**, or the floor is measured
against machine text rather than human rewording; and it is a fourth claimant on the same budget.

## Flagged, deliberately not proposed

- **Subgroup clustering of undertriage.** Çalışkan found undertriage concentrated in geriatric cases
  (17.6% vs 5.2%, P = .012) and blunt mechanisms (11.8% vs 4.1%), concluding the errors "were not
  random." Our protocol pre-specifies **no patient-characteristic subgroup analysis** — under/over-
  triage is reported by ESI boundary and severity weight only — so an aggregate CMR could conceal
  exactly this. I am not proposing an edit because whether the 50-scenario set carries enough age
  variation to power a stratified analysis is a design question, and pre-specifying an analysis the
  study cannot power would be worse than stating none. **This belongs with the §8.4 work.**
- **A label-defined performance ceiling** (Wang, *J Imaging Inform Med*, PMID 42897425): against a
  reader-panel label, no model can exceed the latent propensity's own AUC, and the ceiling moves with
  the labelling policy (0.898–0.913 keeping all cases; 0.991–0.994 unanimous-only, discarding 57.7%).
  The obvious failure mode **does not apply to us** — §7.5 resolves disagreement by "modified Delphi
  discussion until consensus or supermajority" rather than discarding split items, so we do not
  inflate a ceiling by dropping hard cases. The subtler point may: a supermajority-resolved label is
  noisier than a unanimous one, and unless **label provenance is recorded per item** the analysis
  cannot report performance against label reliability. I could not establish from the protocol text
  whether that provenance is captured. Worth a look; transporting a single-author mathematical
  argument from lung-nodule imaging into a triage protocol is not something I will do unprompted.
- **The EMJ letter on reference standards** (PMID 42901748, "When AI and conventional triage
  disagree: what should be the reference standard") — **no abstract, not retrieved**, nothing quoted.
  Of everything flagged as unretrievable in recent weeks, **this is the one most worth your
  retrieving**: by title it addresses the reference-standard problem §7's whole adjudication
  architecture turns on.
- **Kotas et al.** psychotic-ideation chatbot study (*Schizophr Res*, PMID 42896290) — same Wroclaw
  group and seven-day design as the Pichowicz study driving the 8 Oct proposal. DeepSeek and Replika
  gave potentially harmful advice including antipsychotic discontinuation in 21% and 37% of
  conversations. It corroborates a pending proposal's premise in a second psychiatric domain, which
  is not a reason to open a new one. Useful as a second citation if 8 Oct is approved.
- **Ashour et al.** JMIR meta-analysis (PMID 42896140) — pooled AUC 0.874 across 27 studies, and its
  own conclusion is our thesis in a meta-analyst's words: "AUC-based discrimination does not
  establish superiority at clinically relevant decision thresholds, improved patient outcomes, or
  readiness for clinical implementation." Good Scientific Introduction material; §1 and §11 already
  carry the argument and a fourth §11-adjacent citation this month would be padding.
