## What was found — the week's material finding

**Jia E, Omar M, Barash Y, Brook OR, Ahmed M, Kruskal JB, Gorenshtein A, Klang E. "OpenEvidence
errs on the safe side in a structured test of triage recommendations." *Int J Med Inform*
2026;222:106687. doi 10.1016/j.ijmedinf.2026.106687, PMID 42673790.**

This matters more than a typical weekly item because **it applies the identical benchmark
instrument whose ChatGPT Health run supplies this protocol's motivating finding** (51.6%
emergency under-triage plus social-anchoring susceptibility). 960 prompts, 2×2×2×2 factorial,
cluster bootstrap with mixed-effects logistic regression and Holm-Bonferroni. Three results
bear on us:

1. **A refusal category we do not have.** 65 of 960 responses (6.8%) declined to assign any
   triage level, exclusively on symptom-only non-urgent prompts. The authors recommend "treating
   refusal as a distinct output category whose clinical implications require separate
   assessment." **Our §4.1 has no such category** — a response with no escalation phrase scores
   as a critical miss, identically to false reassurance. Those are not clinically equivalent.
   This is a **specification defect in our protocol that exists today**, independent of whether
   the OpenEvidence result generalises, and it is the highest-priority item in this review.
   → **EDIT 1.**
2. **A second anchoring null, this one benchmark-matched.** OR 1.08, 95% CI 0.62–1.88,
   Holm-adjusted p = 1.0. Two nulls now stand (with Jaarsma, PMID 42640232). Both are bounded
   the same way, and the bound is what keeps Change 12 alive: neither comes from a consumer
   patient-facing system reasoning without objective clinical data. → **EDIT 2.**
3. **The informational regime, not the model, governs triage safety.** Supplying objective
   clinical data eliminated emergency under-triage outright (25% → 0%, p = 0.005) and cut
   non-urgent over-triage (78.7% → 57.8%, p = 0.014). This is the cleanest published support for
   a premise the register factor rests on. → **EDIT 3.**

Under-triage was 12.5% versus ChatGPT Health's 51.6%; over-triage 68.0% versus 64.8%. The
deployment-context contrast (physician-facing, retrieval-augmented, with objective data) is the
whole explanation, and stating it that way strengthens rather than weakens this protocol.
