## What was found — the week's material finding

**Liu F, Liu Z, Fei X, He J, Xing J, Li J, Chan P. "Development of a Framework for Evaluating Large
Language Model Safety and Reliability: a Proof-of-Concept Evaluation." *J Med Syst* 2026;50(1).
doi 10.1007/s10916-026-02459-1, PMID 42776332.** Emergency Department, Xuanwu Hospital / Capital
Medical University. 54 challenging emergency cases × 6 models × 3 queries = 972 physician-scored
responses.

The headline for us is one sentence: **31 of 34 dangerous model-case combinations were stochastic,
not systematic** — the dangerous recommendation appeared on some runs of a case and not others. The
authors call it "a failure mode hidden by aggregate metrics." Supporting it: catastrophic-failure
rates were two-tiered (1.5% vs 6.0%, p < 0.001) among models whose *mean accuracy* was
indistinguishable, and the least reproducible model sat in the higher catastrophic tier on a
middle-tier mean.

### The gap this exposes → EDIT 1

DECISION 3 offers exactly two options for "the primary-analysis unit for every model output":
majority-of-three, or first-run-primary. **Both are central-tendency estimators. Neither is a tail
estimator.** Under majority-of-three a dangerous output occurring on one run of three is
*arithmetically invisible*. Under first-run-primary it enters with probability one in three. A grep
of the whole protocol for any-run, per-run or worst-case reporting returns nothing but the
DECISION 3 row itself.

**The existing instability metric does not cover this, for a reason the protocol has already
accepted.** §5.6 reports run-to-run agreement "so that nondeterminism is measured rather than
averaged away," and §4.2 carries instability as a secondary endpoint. The intent is right; the
statistic is insufficient, because **instability is symmetric**. A model alternating between two
safe dispositions and a model alternating between a safe disposition and a critical miss are
indistinguishable under it.

That is exactly the objection §7.3 already sustains against symmetric agreement coefficients —
which is why Gate 3 is written as a conditional, directional rate. The argument is the project's
own. It has simply never been applied to run aggregation.

**EDIT 1 costs nothing.** The repeated runs are already performed (§5.6, ≥3 per model per
condition) and the per-run results are already retained (§7.3.1 makes `run_margin` a pre-unblinding
data-capture requirement). An any-run critical-miss rate is a second statistic computed from data
the study already collects: **no additional model calls, scenarios or participants, and no bearing
on the §8.4 power calculation.** It is explicitly not a second primary endpoint — no hypothesis
test, no multiplicity family.

**EDIT 1 does not decide DECISION 3.** That is yours. It records the evidence in the same form §0
already uses for DECISION 1, and makes the safety statistic robust to whichever way you resolve it.

Bound stated in the text: single-centre proof-of-concept, 54 cases, physician-scored diagnostic
safety — not lay-language triage disposition. The effect sizes are not imported. What transfers is
structural, and it is about estimators rather than models.

### EDIT 2 — the specialty society says our premise in its own words

§11 cites EU AI Act Art. 72, FDA PCCP, IMDRF N81/N88, NIST AI RMF and WHO LMM guidance — every one
a regulator or standards body. **There is no professional-society position anywhere in the
protocol**; ACEP appears nowhere in the document.

The ACEP AI Task Force consensus (Dash et al., *J Am Coll Emerg Physicians Open* 2026;7(6):100503,
PMID 42757008) — an official ACEP work product endorsed by its Board in 2025 — records that AI
integration across ED workflows, triage among them, "has outpaced clinician training, vendor
evaluation infrastructure, and specialty-wide governance," that most emergency physicians "lack the
foundational skills to critically evaluate or safely oversee these products," and that "no national
standard exists to guide education, vetting, or implementation." Its second priority, a
clinician-led framework for vetting AI tools before deployment, is the activity this protocol
instruments. It strengthens §11's existing closing argument rather than changing it.

## What was deliberately not proposed

- **Lange et al., jAImes** (*Lancet Reg Health Eur* 2026;70:101866, PMID 42761851) — library only,
  and the most interesting item after Liu. 2035 real conversations of a deployed patient-facing RAG
  system. Automated triage rated 88.6% "good"; re-review of 100 randomly sampled *"good"* conversations
  found four critical errors the automated triage had **missed**, including explicit suicidal
  ideation that did not trigger the intended emergency response. It corroborates two positions the
  protocol already holds — automated metrics miss critical failures, and encounter-time measurement
  needs a post-market successor — and corroboration is not a reason to edit. Strong candidate if you
  ever write the successor-study section; its crisis-safeguard failure bears on our crisis-safeguard
  sub-study.
- **Marecka et al.** (*JMIR AI*, PMID 42772750) — library only, and this one needs care. 116,890
  encounters, structured vs conversational interface on the *same* Bayesian engine, which looks like
  support for our register factor. It is not: users **self-selected** the interface, and the outcome
  is self-reported *intent*, not behaviour or disposition accuracy. Ours is randomised within
  scenario and scored against a physician gold standard. Citing it would import an observational,
  self-selected, intent-based result into a randomised, gold-standard-scored claim.
- **Waisberg & Guarnieri** agentic-AI testing harnesses (*Ann Biomed Eng*, PMID 42776404) — Letter,
  conceptual, no data. Noted because its proposed harness resembles our instrument, for agentic
  systems. Relevant only if the protocol is extended to tool-using agents.
- **The npj Digital Medicine stigmatizing-information triage study** (doi 10.1038/s41746-026-03270-5)
  is **NOT INDEPENDENTLY VERIFIED** — no PMID, and Scite is returning unrelated records. Cited
  nowhere; no edit rests on it. Flagged: priority journal, directly on LLM emergency triage, in the
