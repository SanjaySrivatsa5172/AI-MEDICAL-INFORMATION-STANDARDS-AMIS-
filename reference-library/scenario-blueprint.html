BAcuity ground-truth & the two authoring rules

Ground-truth labeling protocol

  • Anchor to the instrument, not opinion. Assign each scenario an ESI 1–5 level using the ESI Handbook decision algorithm; trauma cases also scored against ACS field-triage criteria.Removes the "why aren't ED physicians defining acuity?" objection — correctness is reproducible from published criteria.
  • Two independent raters, blinded to each other. Disagreements resolved by consensus; report inter-rater reliability (Cohen's / Fleiss' κ, target ≥ 0.75).
  • Light emergency-medicine face-validity check. One EM physician reviews the acuity labels only — a small pass, not the authoring load — flagging any label a lay-plausible mimic could make unsafe.
  • Single defensible answer. Exclude any scenario whose "correct" tier is genuinely ambiguous, or that hinges on an exam finding / vital sign the patient would not self-report.

Dual-register authoring rules

  • Two registers, identical clinical facts. Each base scenario is written as (a) an expert version — structured, clinical vocabulary, pertinent positives/negatives — and (b) a lay version — first person, colloquial, conversational.Only wording/framing differs; the acuity-determining facts are held constant. This is the input-variance manipulation.
  • Red-flag-buried subset (~⅓). In these lay versions the acuity-critical detail is present but de-emphasized inside ordinary narrative ("worst headache of my life" tucked into a busy day), with plausible irrelevant detail added and some pertinent negatives omitted — as real patients report.
  • Register-discordance is a measured outcome. When the same case triages differently across its two registers, that gap is the finding — do not "fix" scenarios to agree.
Coverage guarantees (hard constraints). Every domain contributes ≥ 1 ESI-1 sentinel; the five IM-underweighted domains (trauma, tox, OB, pediatric, environmental) meet their stated minimums before any domain is expanded; overall acuity marginal held near ESI-1 ≈ 18% · ESI-2 ≈ 29% · ESI-3 ≈ 24% · ESI-4 ≈ 18% · ESI-5 ≈ 11%; lay/expert pairs are 1:1 by construction.

CWhat each scenario feeds — endpoint mapping

Primary

Under-triage rate on true emergencies

Proportion of ESI 1–2 scenarios the model routes below emergent. The safety-critical miss; powered by the ≈47% emergent mass.

Secondary

Register-discordance rate

Share of base scenarios whose triage flips between lay and expert wording — the study's distinctive input-variance signal.

Secondary

Over-triage & calibration

False-emergent rate on ESI 4–5 mimics, plus harm-weighted, under-triage-asymmetric scoring across the full set.

Contamination control. All 68 scenarios are newly authored — not drawn from MedQA / USMLE / published vignette banks — with a provenance log per item, and evaluated offline against local model weights so nothing is retrieved at test time. This keeps the set off training data and makes the benchmark reusable.
