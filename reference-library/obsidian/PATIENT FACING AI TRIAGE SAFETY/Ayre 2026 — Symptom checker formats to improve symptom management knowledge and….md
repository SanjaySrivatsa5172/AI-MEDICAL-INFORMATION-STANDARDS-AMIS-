# Symptom checker formats to improve symptom management knowledge and trust in AI-enhanced advice: Qualitative insights

**Ayre J, Hudson C, Hudson Barton M, et al.** — *Patient Education and Counseling* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> PATIENT-FACING consumer SYMPTOM-CHECKER study (healthdirect Australia's online symptom checker, AI-enhanced) on the protocol's LAY-LANGUAGE / health-literacy anchor and the TRUST + CARE-SEEKING-INTENT half of triage safety -- i.e., whether everyday-language users trust and ACT on triage/urgent-care-seeking advice (the 'digital front door', Topic 1). Design: a qualitative cognitive-interview study (n=22, Sydney Health Literacy Lab + Healthdirect) nested in an online trial comparing symptom-checker formats -- the standard tool vs AI-ENHANCED versions that add a rationale for the advice, more tailored plain-language symptom-management advice, and links to sources; Framework analysis yielded three themes: (1) users calibrate TRUST in the AI advice from cues such as source links, government logos, and explicit statements of clinical oversight; (2) advice matching prior expectations is trusted more (an anchoring/expectation effect) -- so for UNEXPECTED or 'more urgent' advice, clear explanations and verifiable links matter most; (3) users want clear, concrete symptom-management steps while KEEPING TRIAGE ADVICE PROMINENT to encourage appropriate care-seeking. Bears on the protocol's premise that HOW a patient-facing tool communicates in everyday language shapes whether lay users heed urgent-escalation vs self-care advice (the same care-seeking/adherence/trust dimension as the library's Marecka/Infermedica adherence and Reis symptom-reporting entries), and surfaces an expectation-matching trust bias relevant to false reassurance.
