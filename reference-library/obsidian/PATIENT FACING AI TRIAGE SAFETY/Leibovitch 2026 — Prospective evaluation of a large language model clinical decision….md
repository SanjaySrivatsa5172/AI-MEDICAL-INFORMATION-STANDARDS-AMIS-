# Prospective evaluation of a large language model clinical decision support system in the emergency department

**Leibovitch L, Ahituv A, Gorenshtein A, et al.** — *Nature Medicine* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> One of the few PROSPECTIVE in-ED evaluations of an LLM clinical decision support system (Topic 1). DECIDE-AI stage 1 evaluation of SHAKED (a CDSS built on multiple LLMs) in a tertiary emergency department: 1,138 patients over 4 weeks across two parallel units (one using SHAKED, one routine). Findings: clinical adoption declined from 68% to 30% (workload-sensitive disengagement, OR 0.72 per shift hour, 95% CI 0.62-0.83); physicians preferred SHAKED for radiology consultations (OR 2.98, 95% CI 1.58-5.63); NO adverse events, and expert review rated 99/100 sampled outputs clinically appropriate; ED length of stay unchanged (4.9 h both wings, p=0.99); a non-significant trend toward shorter consultation cycle time (-9.4 min, p=0.077). The authors conclude that sustained clinician engagement -- not algorithmic accuracy -- may be the key barrier to effective clinical AI in EDs, and that the results inform randomized trial design but 'do not justify clinical deployment at this stage' (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06902675). Complements the Taylor (NEJM AI) implementation study and Cotte (NEJM AI) real-world study in the real-world-ED-evaluation strand. CAVEAT: clinician-facing ED decision support (not patient-facing / lay-language triage), single tertiary center, stage-1 feasibility.
