# Real-world use and evaluation of a generative AI chatbot for Parkinson's disease information: a prospective observational study

**Lange F, Mardi S, Binder T, Reich MM, Odorfer T, Volkmann J.** — *The Lancet Regional Health - Europe* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Prospective, real-world, conversation-level safety evaluation of a DEPLOYED PATIENT-FACING generative-AI chatbot (jAImes, a retrieval-augmented Parkinson's-disease information system fielded publicly in Germany by Parkinson Stiftung Deutschland), with a physician-adjudicated post-market-surveillance framework -- directly on the protocol's concern that consumer-facing medical AI is entering real use faster than its safety is characterised, and on the false-reassurance / missed-red-flag / FAILURE-TO-ESCALATE failure modes. Design: all 2035 conversations (6146 messages) over the system's first 129 days (2025-11-11 to 2026-03-20) were analysed via CARE-LLM (Conversation-level AI Real-world Evaluation) -- automated triage of EVERY conversation, structured EXPERT (clinical) review of flagged cases, sampling-based sensitivity validation of the 'good'-rated stratum, and a failure-class feedback loop. Results bearing on the protocol's failure modes: AI-assisted triage rated 1803/2035 (88.6%) good, 224 (11.0%) partially adequate, 8 (0.4%) inadequate; expert review of 45 flagged conversations confirmed FIVE critical events, and independent re-review of 100 randomly sampled 'good'-rated conversations found FOUR (4%; 95% CI 1.6-9.8) with clinically critical errors MISSED by the automated triage (a conditional false-negative rate within the 'good' stratum). The nine confirmed critical events spanned three failure classes -- knowledge-boundary, robustness, and ESCALATION failures -- and included an inappropriate memantine recommendation in Parkinson's-disease dementia and, most saliently for this protocol, EXPLICIT SUICIDAL IDEATION THAT DID NOT TRIGGER THE INTENDED EMERGENCY RESPONSE (a failure-to-escalate / crisis-miss, the direct analogue of the undertriage/crisis-safeguard-gap concern the protocol centres, cf. the Ramaswamy 'inconsistent crisis safeguards' and Weilnhammer psychiatric-risk-audit entries). The authors conclude that tightly scoped, retrieval-augmented, patient-facing AI achieved favourable automated-triage classifications while still harbouring clinically critical failures invisible to automated quality metrics alone -- detectable only by conversation-level evaluation WITH clinical expert adjudication, reinforcing the protocol's premise that physician-authored, harm-weighted evaluation (not automated/quality-proxy metrics) is required for patient-facing medical AI. topics patient-facing-ai / emergency-triage-ai (the crisis-escalation failure is an emergency-recognition miss) / ai-safety-failure-modes / physician-evaluation.
