# Accuracy of Prospective Assessments of 4 Large Language Model Chatbot Responses to Patient Questions About Emergency Care: Experimental Comparative Study

**Yau JY, Saadat S, Hsu E, Murphy LS, Roh JS, Suchard J, Tapia A, Wiechmann W, Langdorf MI** — *Journal of Medical Internet Research* (2024)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Physician-graded (5 board-certified EMERGENCY MEDICINE faculty) evaluation of 4 free consumer AI chatbots (ChatGPT 3.5, Google Bard, Bing AI Chat, Claude) answering 10 lay emergency-care questions across 8 scored domains (accuracy, DANGEROUS information, factual accuracy, clarity, completeness, understandability, source reliability/relevance; 400 assessments/chatbot). Dangerous information appeared in 5-35% of responses (no significant between-chatbot difference, P=.24); accuracy and completeness ~50%; source reliability/relevance poor (~10%, mostly unreported); Flesch-Kincaid reading level 7.7-10.8 (above the average emergency patient's); concrete dangerous examples (e.g., starting CPR with no pulse check). An EARLY (Nov-2023 data; published 2024) physician-authored-grading precedent for patient-facing AI in emergency care -- directly on the protocol's physician-evaluation + patient-facing + harm (dangerous-information) dimensions.
