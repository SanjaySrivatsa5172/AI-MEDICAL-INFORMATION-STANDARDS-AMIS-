# Technological Integration in Triage With AI and Machine Learning Compared With Conventional Triage and Narrative Synthesis of Telehealth for Patient Prioritization in Emergency and Acute Care Settings: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

**Ashour FOM, Sami W, Alrimawi I, Rizvi MR, El-Banna MM** — *Journal of Medical Internet Research* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> SYSTEMATIC REVIEW + META-ANALYSIS (Qatar University / Georgetown) of AI- and ML-assisted triage versus conventional triage, with a parallel narrative synthesis of tele-triage and PATIENT-FACING tools, across emergency and acute-care settings (studies 2010-2025; PubMed/CINAHL/Web of Science/Scopus/preprints/registries; duplicate screening; design-appropriate risk-of-bias). 27 studies (13 quantitative, 14 narrative). Four adult-ED studies on critical-care/deterioration outcomes gave high pooled discrimination (AUC 0.874, 95% CI 0.845-0.898, I²=94.4%), with within-study comparisons favoring AI (AUC differences 0.065-0.120). FAILURE-MODE framing central to the protocol: exploratory per-10,000-ED-visit scenario modeling projected 12-38 FEWER MISSED CRITICAL-ILLNESS cases but 45-120 ADDITIONAL FALSE-POSITIVE escalations (the undertriage-vs-overtriage harm tradeoff), and PATIENT-FACING symptom checkers were found to favor sensitivity over specificity. Authors stress that AUC discrimination does NOT establish decision-threshold superiority, improved patient outcomes, or implementation readiness -- citing incomplete threshold/calibration reporting, limited external validation, and sparse pediatric evidence -- and call for prospective multicenter validation, subgroup calibration, equity auditing, and economic evaluation before routine use. Relevant via emergency-triage + the over/undertriage safety tradeoff (failure-mode) + explicit patient-facing symptom-checker coverage.
