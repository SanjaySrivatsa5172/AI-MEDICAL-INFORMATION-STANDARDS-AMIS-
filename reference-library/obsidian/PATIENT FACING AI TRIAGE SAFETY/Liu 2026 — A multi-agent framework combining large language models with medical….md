# A multi-agent framework combining large language models with medical flowcharts for self-triage

**Liu Y, ..., Wang E** — *Nature Health* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Nature Health (April 2026; UC San Diego-led; first author Yujia Liu, senior author Edward Wang) patient-facing self-triage chatbot: three LLM agents select one of 100 AMA clinician-validated step-by-step medical flowcharts from the patient's free-text description, interpret free-text answers, and rephrase clinical questions in plain language; outputs a self-care / see-a-provider / emergency-care disposition. An earlier version is arXiv preprint 2511.12439 (same group; code at github.com/digihealthucsd/Multi-agent-self-triage-system). Accuracy is simulation-only and varies by source: the preprint reports 95.29% top-3 flowchart-retrieval (N=2,000) and 99.10% flowchart-navigation (N=37,200 simulated conversations), while press coverage of the published version cites ~84% correct-flowchart selection over 30,000+ simulated cases with >99% decision-step accuracy; no real-patient or physician-adjudicated emergency-recognition evaluation. Directly on the core-question anchor (lay-language emergency recognition) but as a protocol-grounded MITIGATION/contrast (flowchart-constrained self-triage) to unconstrained consumer chatbots rather than a physician-authored evaluation; ancillary, design_overlap false -- but a candidate for PI design_overlap escalation given the strong patient-facing-emergency-triage topical match.
