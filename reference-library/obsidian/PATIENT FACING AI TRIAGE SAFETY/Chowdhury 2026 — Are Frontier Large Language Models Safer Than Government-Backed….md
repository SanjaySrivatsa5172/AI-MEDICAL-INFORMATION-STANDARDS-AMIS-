# Are Frontier Large Language Models Safer Than Government-Backed Symptom Checkers for Clinical Self-Triage? A Standardised Vignette Evaluation

**Chowdhury AR, Chowdhury B** — *medRxiv* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Preprint (medRxiv, not peer reviewed): Australia's government-backed Healthdirect symptom checker vs six ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini configurations (free and paid tiers) on 45 standardised Semigran-style vignettes (15 emergency / 15 non-emergent / 15 self-care), with the LLMs given FIRST-PERSON patient-language prompts. Reported triage accuracy 48.9% (Healthdirect) vs 73.3-86.7% (LLMs); emergency sensitivity 46.7% vs 80.0-86.7%; Healthdirect logged 2 critical misses vs 0 for the LLMs across 270 evaluations; paid tiers gave no significant benefit; the authors note the comparison is not strictly like-for-like (Healthdirect ran in a more cautious interactive mode vs single-prompt LLMs). Patient-facing self-triage on the protocol's emergency-sensitivity / critical-miss endpoint with lay first-person input -- bears on the digital-front-door premise; direction here is LLMs-safer-than-the-government-tool.
