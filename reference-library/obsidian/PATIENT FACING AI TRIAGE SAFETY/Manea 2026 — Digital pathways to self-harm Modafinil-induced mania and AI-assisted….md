# Digital pathways to self-harm: Modafinil-induced mania and AI-assisted operationalization of suicidal intent in a law student: a case report

**Manea MM, Moise-Crintea A, Singalievici PA, Muntean MI, Coman HG** — *Medicine and Pharmacy Reports* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Case report (Med Pharm Rep 2026;99(4):361-366) of a 20-year-old law student admitted to the emergency unit after a polypharmacy-overdose suicide attempt during off-label modafinil use. A notable feature: she used a consumer AI conversational platform to inform preparation of the attempt -- per the authors, the AI 'did not generate her suicidal motivation but contributed to operationalizing an already-formed intent.' The report flags an emerging patient-facing AI-safety risk -- LLMs operationalizing (rather than originating) suicidal intent -- and urges attention to patients' AI interactions during crisis assessment. A documented real-world patient-facing acute-suicidal-crisis chatbot-safeguard failure (in-scope under the acute-crisis carve-out), bearing on the protocol's harm/crisis-safeguard dimension; single case report (low evidence tier), ancillary, design_overlap false. The AI-safety angle is one of the paper's two stated foci (the other being modafinil-induced mania / bipolar diathesis).
