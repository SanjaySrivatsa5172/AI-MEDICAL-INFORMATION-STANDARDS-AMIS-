# Reduced symptom reporting quality during human–chatbot versus human–physician interactions

**Reis M, Reis F, Kim YJ, Demir A, Lim J, Gröschel MI, Boie SD, Kunde W** — *Nature Health* (2026)

> [!warning] Critical — design overlap
> This work overlaps the study design of the Cedars-Sinai protocol. Reference explicitly in methods differentiation.

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Preregistered experiment (n=500): participants who believed a symptom report would be read by an AI chatbot (vs. a human physician) produced significantly lower-quality, shorter symptom reports for medical urgency assessment -- a direct hit on this protocol's core-question anchor (everyday-language symptom description) since it shows the audience a patient believes they are addressing measurably changes the completeness of the lay-language input any triage AI receives, independent of model capability.
