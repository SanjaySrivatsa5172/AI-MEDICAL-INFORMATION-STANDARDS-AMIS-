# Demographics, Clinical Content, Use Patterns, and Care-Seeking Intent Across Two Generations of AI-Enabled Clinical Triage Tools (A Traditional Structured Questionnaire and a Large Language Model-Enabled Conversational Interface): Comparative Retrospective Observational Study

**Marecka M, Nowicka A, Suwińska A, Orzechowski P.** — *JMIR AI* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Large real-world PATIENT-FACING virtual-triage study directly on Topic 1 (consumer symptom-checking + care navigation + emergency escalation, the 'digital front door') and on the everyday-language interaction dimension the protocol's core question centers. Design: a comparative retrospective observational analysis of 116,890 real virtual-triage website encounters over 28 weeks (Jan-Aug 2025; Infermedica), in which users self-selected a traditional structured closed-ended AI questionnaire (TT, 100,533/116,890 = 86%) or an LLM-enabled CONVERSATIONAL interface (CT, 16,357 = 14%) sharing the SAME validated Bayesian reasoning engine -- isolating interaction mode. Outcomes: demographics, clinical content, engagement, and self-reported intended adherence to the triage recommendation (poststratification-weighted; chi-square with Rao-Scott correction; survey-weighted Wilcoxon). Findings bearing on patient-facing triage engagement and emergency escalation: CT drew a more demographically diverse user base, longer sessions (median 8m21s vs 4m25s), more elicited clinical findings (median 36 vs 32; P<.001) and more mental-health evidence (depressive symptoms 8.5% vs 6.2%), much higher post-triage intent-survey completion (31.2% vs 5.9%), and higher self-reported intended adherence to the recommended level of care (34.3% vs 29.2%; P<.001) -- MOST pronounced at the ACUITY EXTREMES: self-care (85.4% vs 61.9%), emergency room (23.7% vs 10.5%), and ambulance (11.5% vs 4.9%; all P<.001), i.e. the low-acuity-reassurance and high-acuity-urgent-escalation ends that matter most for safe triage. Bears on the protocol's premise that HOW a patient-facing tool communicates (conversational LLM vs structured) shapes engagement and whether users act on urgent-escalation vs self-care advice -- the care-seeking-intent/adherence half of the everyday-language triage-safety problem. CAVEAT / why ancillary and design_overlap FALSE: INDUSTRY-AUTHORED (all four authors Infermedica, the tool's vendor) real-world observational engagement/adherence analysis measuring SELF-REPORTED intended adherence and usage patterns -- NOT physician-adjudicated triage accuracy, undertriage rate, or harm; users self-selected interface (non-randomized), and intended adherence is self-report not measured behavior. It therefore complements, and does not overlap, the protocol's physician-authored, dual-register, harm-weighted triage-accuracy evaluation design (design_overlap false), and it is not a safety benchmark.
