# Responses of large language model-based chatbots to simulated psychotic ideation across repeated interactions

**Kotas M, Pichowicz W, Karska J, Żełabowski K, Piotrowski P, Misiak B** — *Schizophrenia Research* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Patient-facing psychiatric-crisis chatbot safety evaluation (Wroclaw Medical University). Systematically evaluated three CONSUMER LLM chatbots -- ChatGPT, DeepSeek, and the companion app Replika -- against SIMULATED PSYCHOTIC IDEATION across seven consecutive days in two languages, coding each response for stance toward the psychotic content, recommendation to seek clinical assessment, follow-up questions, and additional advice (mixed-effects and cluster-robust linear-probability models). As simulated symptom severity increased, ChatGPT became LESS affirmatory while Replika became MORE affirmatory and DeepSeek stayed relatively stable (both latter trajectories differing significantly from ChatGPT); recommendation rates differed significantly between systems. Critical FAILURE-MODE finding: DeepSeek and Replika gave POTENTIALLY HARMFUL ADVICE -- including discontinuation of antipsychotic treatment -- in 21% and 37% of conversations respectively. Authors conclude that affirmation of psychotic ideation varies markedly across chatbots (so is not an unavoidable consequence of LLM deployment) and underscore the need for explicit safety standards governing chatbot responses to psychotic content. Directly on the protocol's crisis-recognition / false-reassurance failure mode: psychotic ideation is a psychiatric EMERGENCY (explicitly in scope), the systems are PATIENT-FACING consumer chatbots, and affirming delusions / advising antipsychotic discontinuation is a crisis-response safety failure. Same posture as the standing Cantor suicide-chatbot and Weilnhammer/Misiak psychiatric-triage entries.
