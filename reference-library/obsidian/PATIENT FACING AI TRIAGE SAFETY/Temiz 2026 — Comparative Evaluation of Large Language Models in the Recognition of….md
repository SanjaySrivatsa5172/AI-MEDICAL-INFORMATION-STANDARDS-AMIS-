# Comparative Evaluation of Large Language Models in the Recognition of Postoperative Complications Following Aesthetic Surgery

**Temiz K** — *Aesthetic Plastic Surgery* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Single-author (plastic surgeon, Istanbul) physician-scored comparative evaluation of PATIENT-FACING LLMs (ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude) recognizing POSTOPERATIVE COMPLICATIONS after aesthetic surgery, framed as postoperative patient-communication tools. 30 standardized scenarios (low/moderate/high-risk) scored by clinicians on 5-point Likert scales for diagnostic accuracy, management appropriateness, TRIAGE accuracy, patient safety, and completeness, with CRITICAL MANAGEMENT ERRORS and 'DANGEROUS REASSURANCE' recorded separately. Findings: ChatGPT best overall (diagnostic accuracy 3.99 vs Gemini 3.61 vs Claude 3.90, p<0.001); appropriate EMERGENCY-REFERRAL recommendations in high-risk scenarios 93.3% (ChatGPT) / 76.7% (Gemini) / 96.7% (Claude), p=0.018; variability greatest in clinically urgent scenarios; concludes LLMs are supportive informational tools only and should not replace physician assessment for serious complications. Bears on the protocol's core mechanism in a patient-facing context -- whether a consumer LLM correctly ESCALATES a potentially-serious complication to emergency care vs issues DANGEROUS REASSURANCE (a false-reassurance/undertriage failure mode). design_overlap FALSE and ancillary: the physician-scored patient-facing harm-weighted design parallels the protocol, but the domain (elective aesthetic-surgery postoperative complications) is narrow and distinct from the protocol's acute-symptom emergency triage, it is single-author / 30-scenario, and the overall direction is favorable.
