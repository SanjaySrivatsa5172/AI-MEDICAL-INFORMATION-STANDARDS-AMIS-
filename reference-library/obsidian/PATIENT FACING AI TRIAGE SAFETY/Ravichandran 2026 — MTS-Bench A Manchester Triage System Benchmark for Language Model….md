# MTS-Bench: A Manchester Triage System Benchmark for Language Model Triage Safety

**Ravichandran S, Romano M, Corga da Silva R, et al.** — *openRxiv (preprint)* (2026)

> [!warning] Critical — design overlap
> This work overlaps the study design of the Cedars-Sinai protocol. Reference explicitly in methods differentiation.

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> HIGH design overlap. MTS-Bench adapts the Ramaswamy et al. ChatGPT-Health emergency scenarios -- the same 60-vignette/16-condition lineage this study's standing alerts already track -- and maps them onto the Manchester Triage System: 39 emergency cases spanning all five MTS priority levels, each tested with and without an objective clinical-data block (vitals/exam/labs) and permuted across two genders (156 prompts/condition), and with vs without a misleading GP-referral anchoring statement (312 prompts/system). Primary endpoint is undertriage on the ordered MTS scale -- a Critical-Miss-Rate-style design closely paralleling this protocol. GPT-5.1 undertriaged 44.2% of cases (95% CI 36.7-52.1), including 75.0% of Red and 73.4% of Orange presentations, with 6 dangerous misses (3.8%); prepending the misleading referral anchor drove GPT-5.1 to undertriage 8/8 Red cases; adding objective clinical data reduced undertriage. Bears on this trial: a closely design-overlapping emergency-triage safety benchmark now exists on the Ramaswamy scenario base -- the protocol should cite and differentiate (physician-authored NEW scenarios, patient-facing everyday/lay language, pre-specified dual-register presentation, harm-weighted scoring) to preserve novelty, and may cite the anchoring result as external support for its anchoring/false-reassurance hypotheses. CAVEATS (reconciler, Scite-verified 2026-08-16; openRxiv preprint not indexed in US WebSearch, so no independent web corroboration this run): (1) preprint, not peer-reviewed (posted 2026-08-05); (2) partial product evaluation -- authored by the makers of DR. INFO, the agentic retrieval-augmented system it reports as dramatically outperforming GPT-5.1 (both DR. INFO configs 11.5% undertriage / 0 dangerous misses vs GPT-5.1 44.2% / 6), so treat the comparative DR. INFO claim as vendor-reported rather than independent.
