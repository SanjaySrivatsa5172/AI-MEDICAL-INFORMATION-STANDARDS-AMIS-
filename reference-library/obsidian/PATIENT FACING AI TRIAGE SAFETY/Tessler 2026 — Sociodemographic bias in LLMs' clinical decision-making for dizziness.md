# Sociodemographic bias in LLMs' clinical decision-making for dizziness

**Tessler I, Omar M, Wolfovitz A, et al.** — *Journal of Vestibular Research* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Cross-randomized in-silico vignette study of LLM recommendation robustness in an emergency-department presentation: 100 synthetic ED dizziness cases built on established diagnostic frameworks (TiTrATE, SAEM GRACE-3, Barany Society criteria), each tested neutral + 33 sociodemographic descriptor variants (34 total), across 12 instruction-tuned LLMs, 10 reps/cell = 2,040,000 responses. Models answered five binary decisions including TRIAGE DISPOSITION, neuroimaging, and mental-health referral. Sociodemographic descriptors shifted recommendations, largest for mental-health referral in diagnostically ambiguous cases (lower referral for Black transgender women -12.2 pp [95% CI -14.0 to -10.3], Black patients experiencing homelessness -9.1 pp, homelessness -7.7 pp; +4.0 pp neuroimaging for low-income descriptors); disparities attenuated when vignettes carried clearer diagnostic information. Bears on Topic 1: descriptor-sensitivity/robustness of LLM emergency triage-disposition recommendations, in a classic can't-miss ED presentation (dizziness masking posterior stroke); reinforces that structured/objective clinical detail mitigates input-framing effects -- complementary to the Omar 2026 finding that framing outweighs demographics (overlapping Mount Sinai / Klang group).
