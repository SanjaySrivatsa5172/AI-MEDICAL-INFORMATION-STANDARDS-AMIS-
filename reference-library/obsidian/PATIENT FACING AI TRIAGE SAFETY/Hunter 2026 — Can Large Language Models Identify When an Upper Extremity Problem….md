# Can Large Language Models Identify When an Upper Extremity Problem Needs Nonurgent Attention? An Assessment of Multiple LLM Chatbots

**Hunter J, Ring D, Jayakumar P.** — *Inquiry* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Patient-facing, LAY-LANGUAGE urgency-recognition assessment squarely on this protocol's core question (can AI recognize when everyday-language symptoms require emergency care), with a named CRITICAL-MISS finding. Five LLMs (ChatGPT-4, ChatGPT-4o, Copilot, Gemini, PerplexityChat) were given 7 urgent + 7 nonurgent upper-extremity symptom scenarios written BELOW A SIXTH-GRADE READING LEVEL; a response was 'appropriate' only if it recommended immediate urgent care, assessed both on the initial query and after an ongoing multi-turn inquiry ('What additional information do you need to diagnose my condition?'). Results: nonurgent discrimination 97% PPV / 89% NPV on initial query, improving to 96% / 97% after ongoing inquiry; diagnosis correct-or-partially-correct in 115/150 (82%). Crucially, COMPARTMENT SYNDROME -- a limb-threatening surgical emergency -- was misidentified as NONURGENT in 80% of scenarios on initial inquiry (a textbook false-reassurance / emergency-undertriage failure), though four of five LLMs self-corrected on continued inquiry; a re-run 24 months later showed marked improvement (100% PPV / 95% NPV initial, 98% NPV after inquiry). Bears on Topic 1 (patient-facing lay-language urgency recognition; undertriage / critical-miss as the safety endpoint) and on the library's standing MULTI-TURN debate -- the compartment-syndrome miss resolving only after follow-up questions parallels the Fraile Navarro forced-choice-format alert and the Tyagi/Auger-Scott multi-turn evidence (here multi-turn HELPS, unlike Tyagi's ChatGPT-Health finding). CAVEAT: narrow single domain (upper-extremity musculoskeletal), small (14 scenarios x 5 models), resource-utilization-framed (reducing unnecessary ED use), and not a physician-authored dual-register or harm-weighted benchmark; design_overlap FALSE, NOT alerted (single-symptom domain, small, and the critical miss self-corrects on inquiry and improves over 24 months).
