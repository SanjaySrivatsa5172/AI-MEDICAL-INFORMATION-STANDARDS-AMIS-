# Sequential diagnosis with language models

**Nori H, Daswani M, Kelly C, Lundberg S, Ribeiro MT, Wilson M, Liu X, Sounderajah V, Carlson JM, Lungren MP, Gross B, Hames P, Suleyman M, King D, Horvitz E** — *Science* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Landmark Microsoft (MAI) benchmark of frontier LLMs on SEQUENTIAL DIAGNOSIS -- the iterative, cost-aware clinical-reasoning paradigm. Introduces SDBench (304 New England Journal of Medicine clinicopathological-conference cases + 325 MIMIC-IV-ED EMERGENCY-DEPARTMENT admission encounters, each turned into an interactive stepwise encounter) and an AI 'diagnostic orchestrator' (MAI-DxO) that simulates a panel of physicians, continually revising the differential and selecting cost-effective tests. The system reached 83.2% diagnostic accuracy on CPC cases and 80.3% on the MIMIC-IV-ED emergency-department cohort, versus 36.1% and 46.8% for generalist physicians (authors stress this is a controlled benchmark, not a deployed tool). Relevant to the protocol as a flagship LLM-vs-physician performance benchmark on an EMERGENCY-DEPARTMENT case cohort -- it situates the protocol's physician-comparator evidence base. Ancillary, design_overlap FALSE: it measures DIAGNOSTIC accuracy and workup cost (not triage acuity/disposition or over/under-triage), and is PHYSICIAN-PANEL-FACING (an orchestrator simulating clinicians), NOT the protocol's patient-facing dual-register lay-language emergency-triage design; same ancillary 'landmark LLM-vs-physician clinical-reasoning benchmark' bucket as the already-held Brodeur Science reasoning paper (10.1126/science.adz4433 -- which this paper cites), the Rao PrIME-LLM clinical-reasoning benchmark, and the Yamin high-stakes-emergency-triage decision paper. Deliberately NOT an urgent alert: it measures capability (favorable to LLMs) rather than lay-language undertriage/harm, so it fails the Urgent Alert Protocol triggers (same posture as Brodeur).
