# Evaluating Internet-Based AI Chatbots as Unregulated Digital Health Resources in Dental Emergency Management: Comparative Multilingual Study

**Bashah A, Huixin Q, Zaawari H, Al-Waqeerah A, Ghaleb E, Salem A, Wahan N, Chen G** — *JMIR Medical Informatics* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Patient-facing audit of five publicly accessible consumer LLM chatbots (ChatGPT-5.2, Gemini 3.1, DeepSeek-R1, Grok 4.20, Kimi K2.5) on the management of acute MEDICAL emergencies arising in dental practice -- anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest, severe hypoglycemia (life-threatening events that 'can unfold within seconds'). 130 questions across four formats (MCQ, yes/no, open-ended WH, clinical scenario) x two prompting strategies x three languages (English, Arabic, Chinese) over seven days = 27,300 responses, scored correct/incorrect by language-specific expert reviewers. 92.96% correct overall; MCQs highest (94.3%), WH lowest (91.5%); English highest (93.5%); Gemini 3.1 highest (94.8%). Authors conclude the 'presence of incorrect responses highlights potential safety concerns' and that use 'as real-time decision support during dental medical emergencies should be approached with caution.' Relevant via emergency-relevance (acute, time-critical, life-threatening medical emergencies) + patient-facing (publicly accessible consumer chatbots that 'clinicians and patients consult in real time') + AI-safety FAILURE-MODE (incorrect responses as an uncharacterized patient-safety risk in point-of-care emergency decision support). Ancillary, design_overlap FALSE: a management-content-ACCURACY audit via structured multilingual Q&A, NOT the protocol's everyday-language symptom-to-disposition, physician-adjudicated, harm-weighted triage design (same patient-facing-chatbot-emergency-safety bucket as the Khan symptom-checker and Altunpak acute-pancreatitis entries).
