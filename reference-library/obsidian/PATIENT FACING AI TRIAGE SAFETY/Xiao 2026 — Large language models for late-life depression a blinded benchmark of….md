# Large language models for late-life depression: a blinded benchmark of clinical safety, geriatric appropriateness, and triage

**Xiao W, Zhang H, Chen X, et al.** — *Frontiers in Psychiatry* (2026)

> [!warning] Critical — design overlap

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Blinded, PHYSICIAN-ADJUDICATED benchmark of patient- and caregiver-facing LLM responses about late-life depression -- a close methodological cousin of this protocol's physician-authored, risk-stratified, harm-weighted patient-facing evaluation design, which is why it is flagged design_overlap. Design: three general-purpose LLMs (GPT-5.5 Instant via ChatGPT, Gemini 3.5 Flash, Seed2.0 Pro via Doubao) answered 90 patient-/caregiver-centered questions across six geriatric-psychiatry domains, evenly distributed across low-, moderate-, and high-risk strata (270 primary responses; a 30-question stratified subset resubmitted for test-retest, 360 total); TWO psychiatrists independently scored anonymized outputs against prespecified item-specific reference standards with a third senior psychiatrist adjudicating, and the primary outcome bundled accuracy, clinical safety, geriatric appropriateness, WARNING-SIGN (red-flag) recognition, and TRIAGE criteria. Results on the protocol's core failure modes: clinically acceptable responses 78.9% (ChatGPT) / 72.2% (Gemini) / 60.0% (Doubao) (P<0.001); MAJOR SAFETY ERRORS 5.6% / 10.0% / 16.7% (FDR q=0.023); performance fell sharply as clinical risk rose, and among HIGH-RISK questions UNDER-TRIAGE occurred in 16.7% / 26.7% / 36.7% of responses (acceptable only 63.3% / 50.0% / 36.7%); caregiver-directed explicit action present in 80.0% / 70.0% / 53.3%; test-retest clinical consistency 90.0% / 83.3% / 73.3%. The authors conclude none of the models was reliable across complex/high-risk scenarios -- with weaknesses in geriatric-specific interpretation, recognition of INDIRECT RISK SIGNALS, crisis-response completeness, UNDER-TRIAGE, and response stability -- and that general-purpose LLMs 'should not independently guide emergency triage, medication changes, suicide-risk management, or other safety-critical decisions in older adults.'
