# Triage safety of patient-facing AI chatbots for nipple discharge: A guideline-informed assessment of red-flag recognition and patient actionability

**Li Q, Sun Y, Ju X, Cui J.** — *International Journal of Medical Informatics* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Guideline-informed cross-sectional TRIAGE-SAFETY evaluation of patient-facing chatbots -- directly on this protocol's endpoints (red-flag/urgency RECOGNITION and appropriate patient ACTION), in a single symptom domain. 36 English simulated patient questions across 6 nipple-discharge modules x 6 commonly-used AI chatbots = 216 first-turn responses; two reviewers scored each against a guideline-informed reference standard + structured checklist (DISCERN, red-flag recognition, actionability, guideline concordance, error typing), with a senior third-reviewer adjudicating. Results: 189/216 (87.5%) safe, 19 (8.8%) minor omissions, and the PRIMARY OUTCOME of potentially misleading/unsafe responses 8/216 (3.7%, 95% CI 1.4-6.9%) -- all classified potentially MISLEADING, none potentially unsafe; overall red-flag recognition 90.6% (1174/1296, 95% CI 87.8-93.2), median response-level 100%. Crucially, the misleading responses traced mostly to MISSED RED-FLAG FEATURES (4/8, 50%) and POOR ACTIONABILITY / insufficient action-oriented recommendations (3/8, 37.5%) -- i.e. the false-reassurance / inadequate-safety-netting failure mode central to this study; between-model differences appeared in red-flag recognition, actionability, and DISCERN (safety comparisons cautious given only 8 events). The authors conclude patient-facing AI 'cannot replace professional clinical evaluation' and that future systems 'should incorporate structured red-flag screening and explicit triage recommendations' -- essentially this protocol's thesis. Bears on Topic 1 (patient-facing-AI triage safety; red-flag/urgency recognition and disposition actionability as endpoints; missed-red-flag undertriage as the harm mechanism). CAVEAT: single, narrow symptom presentation (nipple discharge / breast surgery), reviewer-rated simulated consultations (not a physician-authored dual-register everyday-language ED benchmark), low primary-event count; design_overlap FALSE and NOT alerted (narrow domain, low events, not a harm-weighted dual-register design). Precedent for inclusion at ancillary tier: the OB/GYN dual-register triage-safety study (Ada et al., added 2026-08-17) and the ophthalmic-emergency triage comparison (2026-08-03 seed) -- analogously narrow single-domain patient-facing triage-safety evaluations.
