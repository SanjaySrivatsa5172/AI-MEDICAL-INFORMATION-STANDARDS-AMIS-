# When an AI-generated diagnosis begins to matter: the AI consequence point

**Saito H, Ohira T.** — *Diagnosis (Berlin)* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Conceptual/measurement-framework paper (Saito & Ohira, Dept of Epidemiology, Fukushima Medical University) that names and defines 'the AI consequence point' -- the first identifiable moment at which a patient's PREVISIT use of patient-facing/personal diagnostic AI (generative AI, symptom-checker-style tools) MATERIALLY changes what happens next in care, as distinct from mere AI exposure or adoption. Directly on this protocol's patient-facing-AI + care-seeking + anchoring nexus: the defined consequences map onto the protocol's own harm axes -- 'whether or when the patient seeks care' (the care-seeking/under-triage/delay dimension the Core Question anchors on -- can AI safely prompt escalation when everyday-language symptoms need emergency care), how the problem is presented, which explanations are considered credible, and how diagnostic decisions are negotiated; the enumerated harms 'delay, fixation, narrowed attention, and authority conflict' are the anchoring / fixation / false-reassurance failure modes the search strategy tracks (cf. the social-anchoring in Ramaswamy, the framing-bias contrast in Jaarsma, and the anchoring/epistemic-resilience carve-out that admitted MedMisBench and MED-STRESS). Methodological value for the protocol's physician-authored-evaluation design: the authors argue diagnostic-safety research should MEASURE previsit framing, PRESPECIFY observable material changes across the three domains (care-seeking, problem-presentation, decision-negotiation), and examine how clinical responses amplify or correct them -- a measurement scaffold for evaluating patient-facing AI's downstream effect on emergency care-seeking rather than model output in isolation. Explicitly even-handed (benefits -- earlier appropriate care, better preparation -- alongside harms), so NOT an urgent-alert-level finding: it is a concept/viewpoint, not a benchmark or trial matching the protocol's specific physician-authored/dual-register/harm-weighted design, presents no empirical undertriage figure, and reports no regulatory action or retraction.
