# Diagnostic and Triage Performance of Large Language Models in Hand and Wrist Complaints: A Standardised Vignette Evaluation

**Counihan CM, Duncan RJD, Bullock MG, Mallina R** — *Hand (N Y)* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Patient-facing consumer-LLM diagnostic + triage audit using fictitious first-person 'patient-voice' vignettes for hand/wrist complaints -- the protocol's everyday-language register. 50 vignettes (20 elective [carpal tunnel, trigger finger, de Quervain, thumb CMC OA], 20 traumatic [mallet finger, scaphoid/metacarpal/distal-radius fractures], 10 minor) entered into three free consumer LLMs (ChatGPT, Google Gemini, Microsoft Copilot); first response scored for primary diagnostic + triage accuracy. Overall diagnostic accuracy 84.7% (96.7% elective / 65.0% trauma / 100% minor); overall triage accuracy 83.3% with 1.3% over-triage and 15.3% UNDER-triage, and under-triage rose with acuity (0% minor -> 16.7% elective -> 21.7% trauma). Authors warn of 'false reassurance and delayed care in time-critical injuries' and conclude LLM outputs should not be relied on for triage, calling for prospective evaluation with real-world patient queries. Relevant via emergency-relevance (triage/disposition accuracy, time-critical fractures, acuity-stratified undertriage) + patient-facing (first-person patient-voice input to consumer chatbots) + AI-safety FAILURE-MODE (undertriage / false reassurance / missed fracture). Ancillary, design_overlap FALSE -- BUT the closest METHODS cousin among recent scan finds: it shares the protocol's patient-voice (lay-language) input + triage-disposition + acuity-weighted undertriage-harm analysis.
