# Sex-based diagnostic bias in AI symptom checkers: a structured, exploratory audit of three platforms

**Khan S, Paasche-Orlow MK** — *Frontiers in Digital Health* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Physician-co-authored (Paasche-Orlow, MD, Tufts) structured equity AUDIT of three widely used PATIENT-FACING consumer AI SYMPTOM CHECKERS -- Ada Health, Isabel Healthcare, and Docus AI -- squarely on the protocol's core question (first-line self-triage: whether and how quickly a person seeks EMERGENCY care). Four standardized EMERGENCY vignettes (acute coronary syndrome WITH chest pressure, ACS WITHOUT chest pressure, pulmonary embolism, panic attack) were entered for matched male/female synthetic profiles with SEX as the sole altered variable. All three platforms showed sex-based divergence in differential-diagnosis rank, assigned probability, and generated advisory text (direction not uniform). Key FAILURE-MODE findings bearing on undertriage / false reassurance: Isabel ranked Heart Attack 1st for the male but 3rd for the female profile on ACS-with-chest-pressure (and 7th vs 5th on the panic vignette); Docus AI assigned a LOWER ACS probability to the female profile on ACS-without-chest-pressure -- despite its own text calling that presentation common in women -- and softened the closing advisory from 'crucial' (male) to merely 'important' (female), an internally self-contradictory justification; Ada's female-profile text stated heart attacks are 'slightly more common in men' alongside a demoted cardiac ranking. Authors argue for pre- and post-deployment equity auditing of symptom checkers attending to both numeric outputs and generated text. Directly on the protocol's patient-facing emergency-triage undertriage / false-reassurance / missed-red-flag failure mode (and its health-equity dimension). Ancillary (a new daily-scan find, not a protocol-packet-cited core reference), design_overlap FALSE: it is a SEX-BIAS equity audit using a small vignette set, not the protocol's dual-register, physician-adjudicated, harm-weighted lay-vs-clinical triage design (a methods cousin, not a close design match).
