# When algorithms triage trauma: Diagnostic accuracy, undertriage risk, and prompt fragility in frontier large language models

**Çalışkan YK, Başak F, Erdem O** — *Surgery* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Physician-adjudicated in-silico BENCHMARK (University of Health Sciences, Istanbul) of 3 FRONTIER LLMs (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3.0 Flash, Claude 4.5 Opus) on TRAUMA TRIAGE, focused squarely on UNDERTRIAGE -- the protocol's central critical-miss concern. 150 trauma vignettes (deidentified educational/registry-informed) emulating secondary triage in a referring-ED setting; locked prompt template; reference standard = consensus adjudication by board-certified SURGEONS using Injury Severity Score-anchored categories. Overall major-trauma sensitivity 89.4%, specificity 86.5%, pooled kappa 0.74 (GPT-5.2 best: AUROC 0.94 / kappa 0.82; Gemini 0.91/0.75; Claude 0.89/0.65). Key FAILURE-MODE findings: undertriage clustered disproportionately in GERIATRIC scenarios (17.6% vs 5.2% in younger adults, P=.012) and in BLUNT vs penetrating trauma (11.8% vs 4.1%, P=.045); PROMPT FRAGILITY -- 11.2% of lexically perturbed prompts changed triage category (max instability 24.5%); simulated undertriage-linked DELAY affected 14.2% of major-trauma cases (mean 18.5 min). Authors conclude frontier LLMs show strong overall discrimination but clinically relevant fragility clustering in 'the very scenarios in which real-world trauma systems already struggle,' supporting use only as SUPERVISED ADJUNCTS rather than autonomous triage agents. Directly on the protocol's undertriage / false-reassurance failure mode and physician-adjudicated harm-weighting.
