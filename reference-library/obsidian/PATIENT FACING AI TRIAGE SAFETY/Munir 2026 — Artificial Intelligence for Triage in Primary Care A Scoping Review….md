# Artificial Intelligence for Triage in Primary Care: A Scoping Review of Clinical Applicability

**Munir L, Cai A, Leslie O, Cai A, Perrotta D, Andrews J, Leslie A, Stretton B, Kovoor JG** — *International Journal of Medical Informatics* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> PRISMA-ScR SCOPING REVIEW (Griffith University / University of Adelaide, Australia; 17 studies 2013-2026, 12 countries) mapping AI TRIAGE systems in PRIMARY CARE by triage function and by whether they are patient-facing, clinician-facing, or automated. Most (14/17) were clinician-facing decision support, THREE had a PATIENT-FACING component, and only TWO performed urgency classification or SELF-TRIAGE; none autonomous. Diagnostic/screening triage was the most common function (7), then prioritisation (4) and referral routing (4). As a clinician aid AI improved performance (e.g., dermoscopy management sensitivity 72.9%->94.2%), but standalone models sometimes fell below clinicians; reported accuracy 41.2-98.28%, AUROC 0.65-0.90 on heterogeneous outcomes; validation mostly internal (11/17), external in 4, NONE prospective-randomised or outcome-measuring. Authors judge the evidence base immature (unreliable reference standards, sparse equity assessment, absent prospective outcome data) and call for outcome-anchored evaluation attentive to SAFETY and EQUITY. Relevant to the protocol as an on-topic methodological map of patient-facing vs clinician-facing AI triage and the thinness of the safety/equity evidence.
