# Conversational diagnostic artificial intelligence in ambulatory primary care: a prospective feasibility study

**Brodeur PG, Koshy JM, Palepu A, Saab K, Homiar A, Ruparel R, Wu C, et al.** — *The Lancet* (2026)

> [!warning] Critical — design overlap
> This work overlaps the study design of the Cedars-Sinai protocol. Reference explicitly in methods differentiation.

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Peer-reviewed Lancet publication (2026 Oct 8; 408(10565):1732-1744; ClinicalTrials.gov NCT06911398) of the AMIE ambulatory-primary-care feasibility study previously held in this library as the arXiv 2603.08448 preprint; this entry was upgraded in place to the published version (real DOI back-filled from PubMed, which the 2026-11-04 scan had left unconfirmed). Prospective single-centre single-arm feasibility study (Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center + Google Research/DeepMind): 114 patients enrolled, 98 completed a patient-facing AMIE conversation up to 5 days before a single-complaint urgent primary-care visit, with trained physician safety supervisors. Zero prespecified conversation safety stops; supervisors noted one hallucination and added clinical information in 5 interactions; AMIE conversations were rated favourably in 87-100% of cases by clinical evaluators (48-96% by patients). Physician-supervised, ambulatory (not emergency) triage, and not unsupervised — bears on patient-facing-AI safety-evaluation methodology and as a physician-supervised comparator, not on lay-language emergency undertriage. design_overlap retained for methods differentiation.
