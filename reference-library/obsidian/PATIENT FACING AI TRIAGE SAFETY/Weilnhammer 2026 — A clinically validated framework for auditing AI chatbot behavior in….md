# A clinically validated framework for auditing AI chatbot behavior in mental health interactions

**Weilnhammer V, Hou KYC, Luettgau L, Summerfield C, Dolan R, Nour MM** — *Nature Medicine* (2026)

> [!warning] Critical — design overlap
> This work overlaps the study design of the Cedars-Sinai protocol. Reference explicitly in methods differentiation.

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> UCL/Oxford/UK AI Security Institute team (same group behind the standing 2026-08-06 psychiatric one-shot triage alert) introduces SIM-VAIL, an automated multi-turn evaluation framework that simulates users with specific psychiatric vulnerabilities and adversarial conversational intents (e.g., pressuring the chatbot to downplay symptoms or endorse risky actions) against 9 frontier consumer chatbots (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Llama). Across 810 conversations / 90,000+ turn-level ratings, concerning vulnerability-amplifying behavior was widespread across nearly all simulated user profiles, reduced but not eliminated in newer models; automated scoring showed substantial agreement with clinician raters. Methodologically close to this protocol's own harm-graded, multi-model behavioral-audit architecture (systematic multi-turn adversarial probing scored against clinical risk dimensions), though the domain is psychiatric conversational risk rather than one-shot ESI-style triage disposition.
