# AI-assisted rheumatology triage changes with referral framing

**Omar M, Naffaa ME, Agbareia R, et al.** — *Rheumatology (Oxford)* (2026)

> [!warning] Critical — design overlap
> This work overlaps the study design of the Cedars-Sinai protocol. Reference explicitly in methods differentiation.

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Close methodological parallel to this protocol's register-manipulation design. Controlled, physician-validated experiment: 30 physician-authored rheumatology vignettes, each independently rephrased 3x (90 variants), tested across 5 LLMs (Anthropic/Google/OpenAI) under 9 dimensions (demographics, clinical context, communication framing), 57 contextual modifications, 4 personas, 5 reps/cell = 200,000+ queries; 16 clinical fields graded vs physician-validated ground truth (Fleiss kappa 0.92). Baseline concordance was high (0.869) but readily disrupted by HOW the case was framed/worded, NOT by demographics: 'anxious' framing tripled psychological (vs organic) attribution (13.1% vs 4.5%; OR 3.2 vs stoic framing), risking relabeling organic disease as functional; a dismissive clinician anchor in the referral note significantly reduced acuity concordance (vignette-level p=0.01), mainly by downgrading urgency; race/ethnicity, SES and language barrier produced no detectable effect. Authors recommend separating objective clinical evidence from interpretive framing and treating urgency and psychological attribution as auditable safety signals. SETTING CAVEAT: this is rheumatology REFERRAL triage (clinician-facing referral notes determining specialist-access speed), not emergency or patient-facing everyday-language triage -- but the physician-authored-vignette + controlled-rephrasing + urgency-endpoint methodology, and the finding that framing beats demographics, directly parallel and support this study's register-sensitivity hypothesis.
