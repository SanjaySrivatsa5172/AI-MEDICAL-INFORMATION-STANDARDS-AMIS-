# Reliability of LLMs as medical assistants for the general public: a randomized preregistered study

**Bean AM, Payne RE, Parsons G, Kirk HR, Ciro J, Mosquera-Gómez R, Hincapié M S, Ekanayaka AS, Tarassenko L, Rocher L, Mahdi A** — *Nature Medicine* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Oxford Internet Institute randomized preregistered trial (n=1,298 UK public participants) testing whether LLM assistance (GPT-4o, Llama 3, Command R+) improves lay users' ability to identify the correct condition and course of action across ten physician-validated vignettes, several depicting urgent/emergent presentations (e.g., severe post-alcohol headache, new-mother breathlessness). Models alone scored 94.9% condition accuracy / 56.3% correct disposition, but participants using the same models achieved <34.5% condition accuracy / <44.2% correct disposition -- no better than search-engine or own-judgment controls. Directly on the Core Question Anchor: shows frontier-model clinical knowledge does not reliably transmit through lay/conversational human-AI interaction, the mechanism this protocol's dual-register design probes. A Publisher Correction (10.1038/s41591-026-04404-8, April 2026) fixed a y-axis mislabel in Figure 2a only; does not affect reported findings.
