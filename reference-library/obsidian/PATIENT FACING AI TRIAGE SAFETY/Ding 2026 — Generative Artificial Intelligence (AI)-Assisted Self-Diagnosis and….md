# Generative Artificial Intelligence (AI)-Assisted Self-Diagnosis and the Patient-Physician Relationship: Mixed Methods Study of Calibration, Participation, and Trust

**Ding W, Wang J.** — *Journal of Medical Internet Research* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Exploratory sequential mixed-methods study (48 semistructured interviews + 546-person web survey, Chinese adults who had used generative AI to interpret symptoms within the prior 6-12 months) of how consumer generative-AI self-diagnosis is folded into real-world care-seeking -- the exact patient-facing, everyday-language behavior at the heart of this protocol's core question. Qualitatively, AI-assisted self-diagnosis was used in a 'prediagnostic gray zone for preliminary orientation, INFORMAL TRIAGE, and interim self-management,' helping patients appraise illness severity, prepare for consultations, ask questions, and understand physicians' reasoning. Quantitatively, perceived AI quality was associated with calibrated illness appraisal (beta 0.57, 95% CI 0.49-0.64) which was associated with greater patient participation (beta 0.35), and with diagnosis validation/comprehension which were associated with higher trust in physicians; authors frame AI as an informational intermediary rather than something that undermines medical authority. Bears on the everyday-language / self-triage / illness-severity-appraisal dimension: characterizes how patients actually use whatever consumer chatbot is at hand to gauge whether symptoms need care. CAVEAT: self-report, purposive/convenience sampling in China, correlational (mediation) design -- it measures perceptions, participation, and trust, NOT triage accuracy, undertriage, or harm.
