# Large language models exhibit greater diagnostic anchoring than physicians in a forced-choice vignette study

**Sheppert A, Shen C, Geissal E, et al.** — *International Journal of Medical Informatics* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Head-to-head LLM-vs-physician test of the anchoring failure mode that is central to this protocol (anchoring on a plausible benign explanation / false reassurance). Forced-choice ranking over 9 pairs of internal-medicine outpatient vignettes, each pair an anchored version (a PATIENT STATEMENT suggesting a plausible-but-unlikely diagnosis) vs a matched control; 20 PGY1-3 IM residents + 5 attendings (225 responses) vs 8 LLMs (144 outputs). Primary endpoint = proportion ranking the anchor first: LLMs 55.6% (40/72) vs residents 21.2% (17/80) vs attendings 10.0% (2/20); LLMs' odds of ranking the anchor first were ~4x physicians' (vs residents OR 3.94, 95% CI 2.32-7.33; vs attendings OR 4.07, 2.47-7.21; both P<0.001), and LLMs put the anchor in the top-5 in 97.2% of outputs (vs 67.5%/65.0%). Directly supports the study's premise that a lay-phrased benign cue can distort AI reasoning more than clinicians'. Also bears on the standing Fraile Navarro alert (which argues forced-choice exam format inflates LLM triage failures): here, within the SAME forced-choice paradigm, LLMs anchor substantially more than humans, i.e. the format does not equalize them. CAVEAT: internal-medicine differential-diagnosis ranking, not emergency triage-acuity or a harm-weighted endpoint; authors call for open-ended real-world follow-up.
