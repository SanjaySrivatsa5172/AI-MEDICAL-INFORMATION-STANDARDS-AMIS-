# The Reliability of Human Evaluation of Large Language Models in Health Care Settings: Scoping Review

**Yang E, Ko S, Woo H.** — *Journal of Medical Internet Research* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> PRISMA-ScR scoping review (71 studies, Jan 2016-Jul 2025) mapping how HUMAN EVALUATION of clinical LLM reliability is actually operationalized -- methodologically foundational for this protocol's physician-authored-evaluation design. Six reliability indicators recur (accuracy, relevance, completeness, clarity, safety, consistency); clinical-domain evaluations emphasized guideline concordance, internal consistency, and structural coherence, whereas public-health evaluations emphasized understandability, harm potential, and repeat-response consistency; single-specialty clinicians were the most common evaluators, evaluator panels were generally 5 or fewer members, and 5-point Likert scales with researcher-defined rubrics were the common approach. Key limitations across the field: evaluator subjectivity, nonstandardized indicators, and limited evaluation scope/settings -- leading the authors to conclude human LLM evaluation is insufficiently standardized and to call for domain-specific standardized frameworks (indicator definitions, judgment criteria, evaluator guidance, and procedures). Directly informs the study's rubric design, evaluator-panel construction, and indicator standardization. CAVEAT: a scoping review of evaluation METHODOLOGY, not an evaluation of triage performance.
