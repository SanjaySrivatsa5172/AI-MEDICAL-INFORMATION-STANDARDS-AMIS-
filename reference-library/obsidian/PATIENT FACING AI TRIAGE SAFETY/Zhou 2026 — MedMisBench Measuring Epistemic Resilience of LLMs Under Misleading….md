# MedMisBench: Measuring Epistemic Resilience of LLMs Under Misleading Medical Context

**Zhou H, Zou X, Wu J, Wu S, Yu J, Segal BM, et al. (Clifton DA, senior author)** — *arXiv preprint / bioRxiv* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Introduces 'epistemic resilience' (ability to maintain correct medical judgment under adversarial/misleading context) and MedMisBench, a 10,932-item benchmark with 48,889 misleading context-option pairs across medical reasoning, agentic capability, and patient-journey evaluation. Mean accuracy on correctly-answered questions collapses from 71.1% to 38.0% once misleading context is injected (51.5% attack success); authority-framed fabrications and 'spurious anchoring' corruptions are the most damaging injection types. Bears on the search strategy's anchoring/false-reassurance/sycophancy angle -- a lay patient's own framing of symptoms (e.g., minimizing language, a prior benign self-diagnosis volunteered in the message) could function as exactly this kind of misleading context in the everyday-language emergency-recognition scenarios central to this protocol. Not triage-specific; included as ancillary methodology under the clinical-bias filter's anchoring-bias carve-out.
