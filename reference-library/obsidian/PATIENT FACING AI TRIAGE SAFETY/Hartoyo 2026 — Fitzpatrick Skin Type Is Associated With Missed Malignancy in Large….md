# Fitzpatrick Skin Type Is Associated With Missed Malignancy in Large Language Model Dermatologic Diagnosis: Failure Patterns of ChatGPT and Claude

**Hartoyo M, Vattigunta M, Devarajan RS, Kowalczyk W, Nouri K.** — *International Journal of Dermatology* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Research Letter (Hartoyo, Vattigunta, Devarajan, Kowalczyk & Nouri; Dr. Phillip Frost Department of Dermatology and Cutaneous Surgery, University of Miami Miller School of Medicine) reporting FAILURE PATTERNS of two consumer general-purpose LLMs -- ChatGPT and Claude -- in dermatologic diagnosis, with the central finding that MISSED MALIGNANCY is associated with FITZPATRICK SKIN TYPE (a skin-of-color equity disparity in the models' failure to flag malignant lesions). Bears on two protocol themes at once: (1) the MISSED-RED-FLAG / false-reassurance failure mode -- a consumer LLM failing to surface a malignancy (skin cancer) is the dermatologic analogue of the emergency-undertriage / missed-red-flag risk the protocol centers on, in the exact consumer tools (ChatGPT, Claude) patients use to self-check lesions; and (2) EQUITY / demographic bias in patient-facing-AI safety (worse missed-malignancy for darker Fitzpatrick skin types / skin of color), the same fairness axis as the library's EQUITRIAGE gender-bias, intersectional-bias, and pervasive-bias ED-triage entries.
