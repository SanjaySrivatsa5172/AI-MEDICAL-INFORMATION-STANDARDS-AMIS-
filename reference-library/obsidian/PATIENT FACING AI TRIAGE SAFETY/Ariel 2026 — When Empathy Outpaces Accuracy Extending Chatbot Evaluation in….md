# When Empathy Outpaces Accuracy: Extending Chatbot Evaluation in Behavioral Health

**Ariel D, Hasid B** — *NEJM AI* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Physician-authored NEJM AI letter/commentary (family medicine, Clalit Health Services) extending Uscher-Pines et al.'s evaluation of SEVEN consumer chatbots for alcohol-misuse support (empathy consistently strong, information quality not). Its central point is squarely the protocol's thesis: several rater-identified chatbot errors involved REASSURANCE about symptoms that in clinical practice would prompt assessment for ALCOHOL WITHDRAWAL SYNDROME -- a condition carrying risk of SEIZURE and DEATH -- yet the original scoring framework did NOT weight errors by clinical severity; and recent evidence shows PATIENTS CANNOT independently detect harmful advice when delivered with warmth and apparent competence. The authors call for future evaluations to WEIGHT chatbot errors by potential for harm, to examine how out-of-workflow chatbot use may erode physicians' ability to identify at-risk patients, and to assess non-English performance. Relevant via emergency-relevance (alcohol-withdrawal = acute, seizure/death, time-critical symptom recognition) + patient-facing (consumer behavioral-health chatbots) + failure-mode (false reassurance / missed red-flag / sycophancy -- 'harmful advice delivered with warmth').
