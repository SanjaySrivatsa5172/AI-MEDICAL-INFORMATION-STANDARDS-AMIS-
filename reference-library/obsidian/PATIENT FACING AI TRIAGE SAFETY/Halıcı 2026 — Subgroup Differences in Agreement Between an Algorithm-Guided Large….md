# Subgroup Differences in Agreement Between an Algorithm-Guided Large Language Model and Routine Emergency Department Triage

**Halıcı A, Cesur E, Çelik F.** — *Medical Science Monitor* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> ED-triage agreement study whose headline is SUBGROUP UNDERTRIAGE -- directly on Topic 1's undertriage-as-safety-endpoint and the fairness/equity dimension. Retrospective, 1,960 adult ED visits with complete triage data; a standardized prompt (age, sex, chief complaint, comorbidities, systolic/diastolic BP, heart rate, SpO2, temperature, GCS) had the LLM assign one category in a 5-level ESI-based system, compared against routine ED triage. Overall: 71.3% five-level accuracy, substantial agreement (quadratic-weighted kappa 0.824); urgent-vs-non-urgent AUC 0.768, sensitivity 0.630, specificity 0.906. Lower-acuity (potential UNDERTRIAGE) discordance 9.8% and higher-acuity (overtriage) 18.9% -- and, crucially, discordance was NON-UNIFORM across subgroups: lower-acuity/undertriage assignments vs routine triage were more frequent in OLDER ADULTS, TRAUMA, and DIABETES, while infectious presentations showed the highest concordance. That systematic subgroup undertriage pattern is a named safety-failure-mode / equity finding that bears on the protocol's undertriage endpoint and its bias-across-subgroups concern. CAVEAT: the LLM was fed structured clinical data + vitals (a clinician-data decision-support comparison, NOT patient-facing lay-language free text), and the authors are explicit that routine triage was an operational comparator, not a gold standard -- so results reflect agreement with local practice, not definitive accuracy or safety; prospective outcome validation is required. design_overlap FALSE (structured-data agreement study, not a patient-facing dual-register physician-authored eval); NOT alerted.
