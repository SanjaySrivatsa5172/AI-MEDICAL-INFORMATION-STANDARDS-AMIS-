**Rehman T, Jarrett P, Lesko J, et al.** — *Journal of the American College of Emergency Physicians Open* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Emergency-medicine FRAMEWORK/GOVERNANCE paper (11-author multi-institution EM-informatics group incl. R.A. Taylor and P. Jarrett) defining PATIENT-FACING AI (PF-AI) for the emergency department and constraining where it may be used -- squarely on the protocol's policy/framework strand (patient-facing-ai + emergency-triage-ai + policy-regulation). The ED is framed as both a safety net and, increasingly, a 'DIGITAL FRONT DOOR for unscheduled acute care' -- the protocol's exact everyday-language entry point. The authors define PF-AI as tools that interact directly with patients to collect information, deliver tailored communication, and support decision-making UNDER CLINICIAN GOVERNANCE, and present a constrained framework across the emergency care journey (structured intake, patient education, multilingual communication, post-discharge follow-up) while setting STRICT BOUNDARIES given the risks of UNDERTRIAGE, confabulated content, workflow burden, privacy harm, INEQUITABLE ACCESS, cost escalation and medico-legal ambiguity; they map operational touchpoints from pre-arrival to follow-up, specify information-flow and accountability checkpoints, and propose a HUMAN-OVERSIGHT model with defined ESCALATION roles, emphasizing equity, interoperability, usability, privacy, safety verification and auditability -- PF-AI should augment, not replace, clinician judgment. Directly contextualizes the protocol's premise and governance strand (undertriage as a first-class risk of patient-facing ED AI; the digital-front-door framing; clinician-governed escalation).
