# Clinical Safety Thresholds and Decision Risks of Search-Augmented Large Language Models in Acute Pancreatitis: A Guideline-Anchored Evaluation for Surgical Practice

**Altunpak B** — *The American Surgeon* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Guideline-anchored LLM clinical-SAFETY evaluation (Istanbul) in ACUTE PANCREATITIS -- an acute surgical emergency. ChatGPT-5.3 and Gemini 3.0 Flash were queried on 27 consensus-derived clinical scenarios spanning diagnosis, management, intervention, and DISCHARGE DISPOSITION, anchored to the 2025 IAP/APA acute-pancreatitis guidelines (zero-shot); three blinded general surgeons scored accuracy/completeness/Global Quality plus binary error metrics. Response-level HALLUCINATION rates were similar (ChatGPT 25.9% vs Gemini 22.2%), and critically ONE FATAL ERROR -- advocating premature necrosectomy -- occurred in the Gemini cohort (3.7%). Both models wrote at a 12th-grade reading level. Author concludes persistent critical errors + high reading level preclude autonomous deployment, positioning LLMs strictly as SUPERVISED clinical co-pilots. Relevant to the protocol via emergency-relevance (acute, time-critical, point-of-care decision support with a disposition arm) + the AI-safety FAILURE-MODE branch (hallucination with clinical harm, including a fatal recommendation).
