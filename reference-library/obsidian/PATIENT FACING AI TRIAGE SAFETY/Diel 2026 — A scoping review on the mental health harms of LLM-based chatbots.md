# A scoping review on the mental health harms of LLM-based chatbots

**Diel A, Torous J, Cuijpers P, et al.** — *npj Digital Medicine* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> PRISMA-based scoping review (N=3,137 records screened across ACM, IEEE, PubMed, Science.gov, Google Scholar; 119 included) synthesizing the documented and hypothesized mental-health harms of consumer LLM-based chatbots -- a patient-facing-AI-safety reference in a priority journal (npj Digital Medicine; senior authors incl. Torous and Cuijpers). Five harm categories: (1) conceptual works citing chatbot limitations (hallucinations, SYCOPHANCY, bias), data-security issues, and risks in severe/high-risk psychiatric cases (e.g., suicide, psychosis); (2) VIGNETTE studies showing LLM chatbots respond INAPPROPRIATELY to mental-health queries relative to clinical standards; (3) cognitive overreliance associated with reduced cognitive/academic performance; (4) problematic use (emotional/social dependency, withdrawal) correlating with mental-health symptoms; (5) 'AI psychosis' -- chatbots reinforcing/validating delusional beliefs. Bears on this protocol's patient-facing-AI-safety and CRISIS-SAFEGUARDS dimensions: the finding that chatbots mishandle high-risk psychiatric queries against clinical standards parallels the study's concern with false reassurance / failure to recognize emergencies in everyday-language interaction (cf. the standing Weilnhammer emergency-psychiatric-triage alert and the Ramaswamy inconsistent-crisis-safeguards finding). CAVEAT: a scoping review of mental-health harms specifically (not emergency symptom/disposition triage), mixing hypothetical/conceptual with empirical harms.
