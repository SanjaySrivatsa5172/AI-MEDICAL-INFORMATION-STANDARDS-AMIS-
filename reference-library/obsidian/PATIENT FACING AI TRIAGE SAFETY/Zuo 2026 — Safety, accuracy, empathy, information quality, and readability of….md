# Safety, accuracy, empathy, information quality, and readability of publicly accessible LLM-based chatbots for traumatic brain injury and concussion questions: a cross-sectional comparative study

**Zuo X, Zuo H, Zhang M, Zheng W.** — *Frontiers in Public Health* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Blinded, EXPERT-RATED safety/quality audit of PUBLICLY ACCESSIBLE consumer LLM chatbots answering everyday-language questions about traumatic brain injury (TBI) and concussion -- directly on Topic 1 (patient-facing symptom/care-navigation) and the protocol's core false-reassurance / missed-red-flag / UNDER-TRIAGE failure-mode axis, in an emergency-relevant condition (head injury). Design (Heilongjiang University of TCM + Xiamen Humanity Hospital / Fujian Medical University): 65 English-language, public-facing TBI/concussion questions submitted to five consumer chatbots (ChatGPT, Gemini, Copilot, DeepSeek, Doubao) generating 325 responses, scored by FIVE BLINDED INDEPENDENT expert raters using guideline-informed criteria plus established instruments (DISCERN, EQIP, JAMA benchmark criteria, Global Quality Score, readability indices); inter-rater agreement high. Results bearing on the protocol's failure modes: responses rated SAFE only 89.2-95.4% and median accuracy 4.00, but 27 responses were classified POTENTIALLY HARMFUL, driven mainly by UNDER-TRIAGE, OVERLY REASSURING ADVICE regarding imaging findings (false reassurance), PREMATURE RETURN-TO-ACTIVITY OR DRIVING guidance, and INSUFFICIENT PEDIATRIC CAUTION; accuracy differences were statistically significant but small while empathy/quality/transparency/readability varied more by model (DeepSeek most readable). The authors conclude chatbots 'should not replace professional medical evaluation' and explicitly caveat that expert-rated response performance does NOT establish patient comprehension or clinical benefit -- the same measurement-gap the protocol's physician-authored design is built to close. Bears on the protocol's premise that consumer LLMs used for lay self-triage can under-triage and falsely reassure on emergency-relevant symptoms (here, red-flag head-injury guidance), reinforcing the undertriage/false-reassurance endpoints and the need for harm-weighted, physician-authored evaluation over generic quality/readability scores.
