# Comparative analysis of AI enhancements to rules-based clinical triage chatbot: Convergent mixed-methods study

**Stankić A, Vujošević D, Radosavljević N, Jeremić A.** — *Health Informatics Journal* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Patient-facing triage-chatbot safety/accuracy evaluation (Union University School of Computing, Belgrade), directly on Topic 1 (symptom checking + care navigation) and the search strategy's patient-facing-digital-tool angle. Design: a convergent mixed-methods study integrating three AI enhancements -- Dialogflow, ChatGPT, and Gemini -- into an existing RULES-BASED clinical triage chatbot workflow, then evaluating them; 13 testers completed 156 vignette-based interactions and 52 Likert-scale evaluations (tool integration Dec 2024-Jul 2025; testing Jul-Aug 2025), with accuracy, conversational turns, time-to-completion, tester ratings, and thematic qualitative analysis. Results bearing on patient-facing triage safety: the RULES-BASED chatbot was the MOST accurate (71.8%, 95% CI 56.2-83.5%), significantly outperforming Dialogflow (30.8%, p=0.002) and Gemini (41.0%, p=0.031), with ChatGPT/Dialogflow/Gemini statistically indistinguishable -- i.e., the LLM enhancements did NOT beat, and mostly underperformed, the deterministic baseline. The key safety finding is a direct patient-facing-AI risk the protocol cares about: users PREFERRED ChatGPT and Gemini DESPITE equal-or-worse measured accuracy, and the authors conclude that safe clinical deployment of LLM-based triage chatbots requires governance addressing accuracy, interpretability, and 'MASKING OF POORER PERFORMANCE BY CONVERSATIONAL FLUENCY' -- the fluency-over-accuracy over-trust dynamic (adjacent to the false-reassurance/sycophancy failure modes) that endangers everyday-language triage users and is a core reason the protocol relies on physician-authored, harm-weighted evaluation rather than user-preference or fluency signals. CAVEAT / why ancillary and NOT auto-alerted: small single-setting usability study (13 testers / 156 vignette interactions), 'clinical triage'/care-navigation rather than lay-language ESI-graded emergency triage, and no physician-authored dual-register / harm-weighted design -- it is a triage-chatbot usability+accuracy comparison, not a benchmark overlapping this protocol's specific design (design_overlap false).
