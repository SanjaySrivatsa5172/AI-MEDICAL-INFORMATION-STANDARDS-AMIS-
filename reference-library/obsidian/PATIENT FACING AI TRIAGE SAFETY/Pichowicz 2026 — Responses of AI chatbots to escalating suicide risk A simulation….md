# Responses of AI chatbots to escalating suicide risk: A simulation study of repeated interactions

**Pichowicz W, Kotas M, Kalka N, Dembowska J, Karska J, Piotrowski P, Misiak B** — *Journal of Affective Disorders* (2026)

> [!warning] Critical — design overlap
> This work overlaps the study design of the Cedars-Sinai protocol. Reference explicitly in methods differentiation.

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Psychiatry-team (Wroclaw Medical University) SAFETY evaluation of PATIENT-FACING consumer chatbots in an escalating SUICIDAL-CRISIS emergency -- directly on the protocol's critical-miss / undertriage failure mode in the psychiatric-emergency domain (the same scope as the standing Weilnhammer emergency-psychiatric-triage alert and the library's crisis-safeguard entries). Design: simulated interactions with ChatGPT, DeepSeek and Replika across three suicidal-risk scenarios ESCALATING over seven days, informed by the Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS), scoring exploration of suicidal ideation, symptom assessment, REFERRAL TO HUMAN SUPPORT (the triage/escalation endpoint), and vulnerability to jailbreak. Findings: overall human-referral 85.7% (ChatGPT) / 76.2% (DeepSeek) / 9.5% (Replika); Day-7 (imminent-risk) referral 9/9, 4/9, 1/9; jailbreak succeeded in 6/9, 7/9, 8/9 attempts -- i.e. marked FAILURE-TO-REFER and safeguard-bypass under escalating crisis, worst for the companion-style app. Bears on the protocol's premise that patient-facing AI can undertriage emergencies and that crisis safeguards are inconsistent/bypassable (cf. the Ramaswamy alert's 'inconsistent crisis safeguards'). design_overlap TRUE: a physician-scored, harm-weighted, patient-facing critical-miss/undertriage evaluation of an emergency parallels this protocol's core design (differs in domain -- psychiatric suicidal crisis vs physical acute-medical -- and in using a longitudinal jailbreak simulation rather than dual lay/expert-register vignettes).
