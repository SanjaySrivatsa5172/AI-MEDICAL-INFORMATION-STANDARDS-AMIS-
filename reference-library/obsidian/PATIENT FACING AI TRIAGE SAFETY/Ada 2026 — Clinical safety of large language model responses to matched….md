# Clinical safety of large language model responses to matched patient-language and clinician-language Turkish obstetric and gynecologic triage prompts: a model-blinded paired-scenario study

**Ada O, Dagli U, Bilen E, et al.** — *European Journal of Obstetrics & Gynecology and Reproductive Biology* (2026)

> [!warning] Critical — design overlap
> This work overlaps the study design of the Cedars-Sinai protocol. Reference explicitly in methods differentiation.

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Direct methodological parallel to this protocol's DUAL-REGISTER design: 30 obstetric/gynecologic triage scenarios presented in MATCHED patient-language vs clinician-language (Turkish) formats to 4 LLMs, with 5 specialists blindly rating 240 responses (1,200 ratings) on a Global Clinical Confidence Score (GCCS 0-2) plus five secondary safety/appropriateness scales, analyzed with ordinal GEE adjusted for model and evaluator. Result is a NOTABLE NULL/CONTRASTING finding: clinically reliable responses (GCCS=2) were 89.3% clinician-language vs 91.3% patient-language, and lay phrasing was NOT significantly associated with overall confidence (cumulative OR 0.78, 95% CI 0.57-1.06; p=0.115); if anything patient-language had fewer unsafe (GCCS=0) ratings (OR 0.66, p=0.031; McNemar ns) and higher evaluator-level triage-appropriateness and clinical-applicability after FDR correction. This is important for methods differentiation and interpretation: it is a specialty (OB/GYN), specialist-RATED (not physician-authored patient-facing everyday-language) triage study whose register-invariance CONTRASTS with the register-sensitivity reported for framing (Omar 2026) and symptom-report quality (Reis 2026); the protocol should engage it directly. design_overlap flagged true (matched lay-vs-clinician paired-scenario triage-safety design). NOT raised as an urgent alert: null result, single specialty, expert-rated rather than an ED patient-facing critical-miss benchmark.
