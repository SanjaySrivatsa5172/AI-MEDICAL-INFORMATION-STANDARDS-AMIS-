# The Utility of Large Language Models to Assist With Emergency Triage Decisions Within Otolaryngology

**Hack S, Attal R, Steckbeck RJ, Boldes T, Ayeni T, Smith AJ, Sagiv D, Kellermeyer B, Garcia-Lliberos A, Rodriguez-Prado C, Biadsee A, Sebelik M, Roxbury CR, Purnell P, Zalzal HG** — *Otolaryngology–Head and Neck Surgery* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Specialty clinician-to-clinician emergency-referral triage vignette study (Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg 2026;175(3):603-611): 30 ENT emergency-referral scenarios across the urgency spectrum, rated for urgency-appropriateness and explanation quality by 10 blinded otolaryngologists (3-point scale); 4 LLMs (GPT-5, GPT-4, DeepSeek, Grok) vs 4 clinicians (otolaryngology attending/resident, emergency attending/resident). The otolaryngology attending scored highest; GPT-5 was statistically comparable (no significant difference in either domain); GPT-4 out-rated both emergency clinicians; at group level the LLM and otolaryngology cohorts did not differ and both out-rated the emergency clinicians. Referral input is clinician-to-clinician (not lay patient-facing), limiting bearing on the lay-register core question; useful as human-comparator over/under-triage context.
