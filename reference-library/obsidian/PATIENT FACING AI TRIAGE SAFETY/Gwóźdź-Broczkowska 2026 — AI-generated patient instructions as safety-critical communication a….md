# AI-generated patient instructions as safety-critical communication: a provisional evidence-informed framework for clinical deployment

**Gwóźdź-Broczkowska A.** — *International Journal of Medical Informatics* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Narrative/interpretative review that reframes AI-generated patient-facing instructions (discharge summaries, medication explanations, portal messages, plain-language materials) as a SAFETY-CRITICAL informatics intervention rather than a readability tool: patients may act on fluent, incomplete, or contextually unsafe AI text without the clinical knowledge to detect the error, and physician/pharmacist review of such outputs has found omissions, inaccuracies, newly-introduced actions, medication problems, and potentially harmful safety issues (especially in complex discharge contexts). Proposes a seven-domain safety framework -- factual accuracy, clinical completeness, actionability, medication clarity, ESCALATION and safety-netting, health-literacy alignment, and accountability/auditability -- to support implementation, local evaluation, and reviewer assessment, and calls for human verification, traceability, equity testing, and post-deployment monitoring for high-risk outputs. Bears on this protocol's patient-facing-AI-safety framing: the escalation/safety-netting domain is precisely the mechanism against false reassurance / failure to advise urgent care, health-literacy alignment is the everyday-/lay-language dimension, and the finding that physician/pharmacist review catches harms readability metrics miss echoes the study's physician-authored-evaluation rationale. CAVEAT: single-author narrative review proposing a PROVISIONAL framework (explicitly not a formal consensus guideline and not an empirical evaluation); PubMed-verified metadata only -- too recent for Scite indexing this run, so no Scite editorialNotices/citation check was available.
