# Deployment protocol and base model jointly determine residual risk in rule-based clinical LLM supervision

**Wu K, Nagori A, Kamaleswaran R, et al.** — *Research Square (preprint)* (2026)

> [!note] Clinical relevance
> Evaluates a five-gate 'sentinel' rule-based supervisor over five models (Qwen 7B, Llama 8B, Meditron 7B, Gemma 4, Sonnet 4.5) on two clinical safety benchmarks under handoff-on/off protocols; residual safety depended on both deployment protocol and base model (strict violation rates 1.5-49.5%; handoff-off residual risk 0-50%). A multisite extension (Llama 70B, N=2935 encounter-derived scenarios) and HealthBench Hard (N=1000) reached ~0% deploy-violation under supervision. KEY physician-evaluation point for this study: a clinician-adjudication pilot (n=50) separated automated safety-rule compliance from clinical adequacy -- of 20 intercepted outputs none were rated harmful but ALL were flagged for missing critical information, while 5/30 ALLOWED outputs were rated potentially harmful (clinical-reasoning errors beyond the sentinel's scope). Supports the protocol's core thesis that physician-authored/adjudicated evaluation surfaces harms that automated compliance metrics miss.
