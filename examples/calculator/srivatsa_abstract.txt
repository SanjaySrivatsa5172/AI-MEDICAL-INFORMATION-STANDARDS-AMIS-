The Epistemic Risk of AI Over-Reliance in Clinical Medicine: Why Productive Uncertainty Matters
S. Sanjay Srivatsa MD FACC FACP

Abstract
Current enthusiasm for artificial intelligence integration in healthcare overlooks a fundamental epistemological concern: the erosion of productive clinical uncertainty. While AI systems excel at pattern recognition and data processing, they lack the capacity for genuine epistemic doubt that drives sound medical reasoning. This analysis examines empirical evidence of automation bias in medical settings, explores the philosophical foundations of clinical judgment, and proposes practical safeguards to preserve the productive uncertainty essential to all medical practice. Moreover, systematic stress testing of leading multimodal medical AI systems reveals that models that achieve high benchmark scores, often succeed for fundamentally wrong reasons, demonstrating brittle performance under minor perturbations and generating fabricated reasoning that masks underlying shortcut behaviors. We argue that without careful attention to maintaining human epistemic vigilance, unfettered AI integration risks creating overconfident clinical decision-making that paradoxically reduces diagnostic accuracy and patient safety.

This manuscript is a narrative analysis. Limitations of conceptual analysis apply. AI systems deployed in clinical settings should be required to express uncertainty in human-interpretable terms.
